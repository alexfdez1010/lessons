A fund can report a larger multiple because its estimated residual assets rose, without sending the LP another euro. A time-sensitive return can change when funding moves, without improving the underlying investment. A useful report keeps those quantities visible.

Amounts are thousands of EUR. Reuse the reporting case's equal annual LP flows: −30, −20, +10, −15, +40 and +25 at years 0–5. They include the initial fee of 5, the only fund expense; carry and personal tax are zero here. This case has no recallable or in-kind distributions. The remaining commitment of 35 is disclosed separately.

## Split distributed value and residual estimated value

<MCQ client: visible pretest
question={"Which ratio stays unchanged if NAV rises while paid capital and distributions stay fixed?"}
options={[{"text": "DPI, because it uses distributions rather than NAV", "correct": true}, {"text": "RVPI, because residual value is outside its numerator"}, {"text": "TVPI, because all its value has already been paid in cash"}]}
explanation={"Residual NAV affects RVPI and TVPI, while DPI uses distributed value."}
/>

**Paid-in capital**, PI, is cumulative contributions under the reporting convention, not the original commitment. Here it includes the fee paid through the first call: $PI=30+20+15=65$. Distributions $D=10+40+25=75$ are cash received. The residual net NAV estimate $V=20$ remains in the fund.

$$\mathrm{DPI}=\frac{D}{PI},\qquad \mathrm{RVPI}=\frac{V}{PI},\qquad \mathrm{TVPI}=\frac{D+V}{PI}=\mathrm{DPI}+\mathrm{RVPI}.$$

**DPI**, distributions to paid-in, is $75/65=1.153846$ times. It includes returned capital, so it is not a 115.38% profit. **RVPI**, residual value to paid-in, is $20/65=0.307692$. **TVPI**, total value to paid-in, is $95/65=1.461538$. Marked gain relative to contributions is 30, or $30/65=46.153846\%$, without annualising it.

<PrivateFundLab client:visible mode="multiples" title="Change estimated residual value while distributions stay fixed" />

If NAV is revised to 10 with no new cash flows, DPI stays 1.153846, RVPI falls to 0.153846 and TVPI to 1.307692. A multiple does not specify elapsed time: the same cash multiple over two years and ten years represents different timing. Nor does TVPI prove the residual amount can be sold today.

### Check reporting level and denominator

Use LP-level net cash and net residual value consistently. Gross portfolio proceeds, fund net returns and a particular LP's side-letter economics are different levels. [Invest Europe's reporting guidance](https://www.investeurope.eu/investor-reporting-guidelines/performance-measurement-and-reporting/) distinguishes these measures. State how fees, carry, recallables and in-kind amounts are treated before comparing funds. Do not subtract the uncalled 35 from PI or silently call it an expense already incurred.

## Apply money-weighted return to the actual dated LP account

<MCQ client: visible pretest
question={"AddNAV 20 to the year 5 cash 25 for an interim IRR. What has happened economically?"}
options={[{"text": "A synthetic terminal mark, not an actual 45 cash receipt", "correct": true}, {"text": "A guaranteed future sale of every remaining asset today"}, {"text": "A reversal of all earlier negative capital contributions"}]}
explanation={"The measurement device must stay distinct from observed cash."}
/>

The prerequisites define **IRR**, the discount rate setting signed dated NPV to zero. Here we apply it to the LP contract rather than redefining a generic project return. Read the [IRR and conflicting-rankings lesson](/company-financials-and-valuation/irr-and-conflicting-project-rankings) for roots and limitations.

For an **interim marked IRR**, append a synthetic sale of residual NAV at the measurement date. With NAV 20, the last entry is actual distribution 25 plus synthetic 20, or 45. The equation is

$$0=-30-\frac{20}{1+r}+\frac{10}{(1+r)^2}-\frac{15}{(1+r)^3}+\frac{40}{(1+r)^4}+\frac{45}{(1+r)^5}.$$

At 0%, NPV is $-30-20+10-15+40+45=30$. At 10%, it is approximately 4.074920; at 15%, approximately −4.449528. The admissible root for this fixed cash-flow family is $r\approx12.226615\%$ annually. Substituting the root makes the discounted signed total approximately zero.

<PrivateFundLab client:visible mode="irr" title="Recalculate interim IRR under a stated NAV realisation" />

If the 20 NAV is omitted—equivalent to valuing residual assets at zero for this diagnostic—the equation ends with 25 and its rate is approximately 4.758954%. That cash-only calculation is not the completed lifetime return of an ongoing fund. The marked figure is not realised cash either. Later calls, distributions, revised NAV and expenses can change the reported return.

### Keep the measurement date and valuation assumption attached

The synthetic NAV realisation is a measurement device, not a sale or a promise. IRR does not by itself establish the return earned on distributions invested elsewhere, nor a causal measure of manager skill. Multiple or absent roots remain possible for other cash-flow patterns; the lab's root calculation is scoped to the stated family. Use actual day-count conventions for irregular real dates instead of applying this equal-annual-period formula silently.

## Compare the same cash flows with a declared public benchmark

<MCQ client: visible pretest
question={"A public replication must match private withdrawals. What financial constraint needs checking?"}
options={[{"text": "Whether the holdings remain nonnegative without unmodelled financing", "correct": true}, {"text": "Whether private NAV can be spent before the valuation date"}, {"text": "Whether every public and private asset has exactly the same risk"}]}
explanation={"A negative benchmark holding would require another stated convention."}
/>

Choose an invented **EUR total-return index**, including reinvested distributions, with levels 100, 110, 105, 120, 130 and 140 at the same dates. Normalise a synthetic benchmark unit so its EUR price equals the numeric index level. This is a mathematical comparison account, not an assertion that an index itself is directly tradable. The account buys units when the LP contributes and sells them to match distributions. Holdings in the table are **thousands of units**, consistent with cash in thousands of EUR. Fractional units are allowed; additional public trading costs and taxes are zero.

At year 0, 30 buys $30/100=0.3$ thousand units: 30,000 EUR buys 300 units at 100 EUR each. Year 1 adds $20/110=0.181818$ thousand units. Year 2's distribution withdraws $10/105=0.095238$ thousand units, leaving 0.386580 thousand. Continue with the same signed flows; each holding remains nonnegative. After withdrawing the final 25, the account holds approximately 0.0253163503 thousand units, worth $140(0.0253163503)=3.544289$.

Equivalently, accumulate all calls and distributions to year 5 using $I_5/I_t$:

$$FV_C=30\frac{140}{100}+20\frac{140}{110}+15\frac{140}{120}=84.954545,$$

$$FV_D=10\frac{140}{105}+40\frac{140}{130}+25=81.410256.$$

The public remainder is $FV_C-FV_D=3.544289$. Private residual NAV 20 exceeds that by 16.455711 under the mark. Define a cash-flow-matched accumulated-value ratio as $(FV_D+V)/FV_C$: here $101.410256/84.954545=1.193700$. With NAV zero the same ratio is below one, despite exactly the same observed private cash distributions.

### Name the comparison without upgrading its claim

This is a fully specified benchmark calculation, not proof of equal risk, liquidity, leverage or manager skill. The chosen currency, total-return definition, cash dates and fee basis matter. A different index or residual valuation can change the comparison. If matched withdrawals would require negative public holdings, the replication would need an additional financing or shorting convention; our illustrated account never does. Do not label a mechanical value difference “risk-adjusted alpha” without an appropriate model.
