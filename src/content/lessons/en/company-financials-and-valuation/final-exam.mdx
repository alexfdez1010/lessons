**Cleanup.** Payments are −100, +230 and −132 at dates 0,1,2. The last payment remains an obligation in the forecast.

**No Root.** Payments are −100, +50 and −10 at dates 0,1,2. The zero-NPV quadratic has a negative discriminant.

**Later Wealth.** The original project pays −100 at date 0 and +140 at date 1, when it ends. The investor holds the entire receipt until date 2 at zero interest, with no extra contributions, withdrawals, taxes or fees.

## Key takeaways

<Callout variant="success" title="What to keep checking">
Use the statements together: an accounting profit and a cash receipt can occur at different times. Match each ratio to the balances and flows it measures. CCC links inventory, collection and supplier credit, but does not replace a cash forecast. A valuation depends on its assumptions, while accounting warning signs identify questions to investigate rather than proving fraud on their own. For project choices, inspect IRR roots and compare feasible signed cash-flow alternatives at an explicit opportunity-cost rate; a project percentage does not determine later portfolio wealth.
</Callout>
