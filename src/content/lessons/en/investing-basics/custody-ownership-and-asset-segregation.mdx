You bought 20 shares through an investment firm. The firm fails. Two very different questions now matter: what rights and records identify your 20 shares, and what those shares are worth. Returning all 20 does not reverse a fall in their price. Conversely, a screen showing 20 does not establish that all 20 are available to return.

This lesson follows an ordinary, fully paid, **settled** shareholding: securities and payment have completed their agreed exchange. We assume no borrowing, securities lending, collateral transfer, fees, dividends, tax or currency conversion in the arithmetic. Actual arrangements can differ. The legal examples explicitly use **Spain and the EU investment-firm framework**, with a Spanish firm belonging to FOGAIN for the compensation example, checked on 6 November 2026. They are not universal rules for every broker, bank, jurisdiction or product.

## Separate the asset, the investor, the custodian and the record

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="You buy ordinary Company A shares through custody firm B. The **asset** is what you hold: here an ordinary share, a unit of rights in Company A. A share is not a deposit of money with the broker, and custody does not turn it into a share of the broker. A fund unit likewise gives rights in the fund under its terms; it does not put each underlying security directly in your personal name.

The **investor's economic interest** is the exposure to the investment's gains, losses and associated rights under the arrangement. People sometimes call this beneficial ownership. Its precise legal form depends on the jurisdiction and contract; it does not mean your name must appear at every level of a securities register. “Economic interest” here is an explanatory description, not a substitute for establishing legal title.

A **custodian** administers safekeeping and related records. An investment firm may perform that function itself or use other custodians or account providers. A **securities record** identifies such things as the security issue, account holder and quantity at a particular level. An account statement is useful evidence about your recorded position; it is not an independent guarantee that the underlying assets exist or that every legal condition has been met.

### Work a pooled record without losing the client detail

An **omnibus account** combines several clients' positions in an account at one custody level. The intermediary's detailed records must still distinguish those clients under the applicable safeguarding requirements. Individual account segregation at a higher level is another arrangement; neither label alone proves how every insolvency claim will be resolved.

### When to identify what you own

Read the actual product and account agreement: security identifier and class, units, settled versus pending status, legal account holder, custody provider and records used to identify your interest. A contract that merely pays according to a share price can be a claim against its contract provider rather than an ordinary shareholding. Matching a familiar price chart does not settle that distinction. Also check any permission to lend securities, transfer title or grant security rights; those arrangements are outside our ordinary safekeeping case and can change the rights and risks.

## Segregation helps protect client rights; it does not insure market prices

<ThinkReveal client:visible prompt="A failed custody firm returns all 20 shares, each now worth 800 euros rather than the 1,000 paid. Is there a missing-asset shortfall of 4,000 euros?" hint="Reconcile quantities first, then compare prices." text="No units are missing in this stipulated case. The 20 shares are worth 16,000 rather than 20,000, a 4,000 market-value decline. Custody restitution and restoring the purchase price are different obligations." />

**Segregation** means maintaining the required distinction between client assets and the firm's own assets; it can also distinguish clients at particular record levels. **Reconciliation** checks that internal quantities agree with external accounts and records. These arrangements aim to make client rights identifiable and safeguard them, including if the investment firm becomes insolvent—unable to fulfil its relevant financial obligations.

Under the **EU investment-firm safeguarding framework**, Article 2 of Delegated Directive 2017/593 requires distinguishable client/firm records, accurate records, regular reconciliations and appropriate identification of client assets held through third parties. Its requirements also address applicable property/insolvency law and third-country limitations. This is a framework implemented through national rules, not a declaration that any chosen provider or account complies. [Read the EU's client-asset safeguarding provisions](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir_del/2017/593/2022-11-22/eng).

Identified client holdings may be returned or transferred through the applicable process without being treated as an ordinary pool of the firm's proprietary assets. The outcome and timing depend on law, records, asset availability and other rights. Segregation does not promise immediate access, correct records despite fraud, or complete recovery when assets are missing. Nor does it prevent the investment issuer from failing.

### Work the complete-return case

Buy 20 shares at 1,000 euros each: original cost $20\times1000=20,000$. At a deliberately chosen comparison date, each share is worth 800. The reference value of the recorded holding is $20\times800=16,000$.

If all 20 are returned, their reference value is 16,000. **Missing units:0. Reference value of missing units:0.** The price-only change is $20(800-1000)=-4,000$. That price loss is not evidence that the custodian failed to return shares.

### Work the missing-unit case separately

Now stipulate that, **after the applicable recovery and allocation process**, only 18 of that investor's 20 recorded units are returned. We are not deriving how an omnibus shortfall must be allocated under any country's law; the final number 18 is an input.

$$
\text{Units not returned}=20-18=2,
$$
$$
\text{Their reference value}=2\times800=1600,
$$
$$
\text{Returned reference value}=18\times800=14400.
$$

Relative to the initial 20,000 outlay, the difference is $14,400-20,000=-5,600$. It reconciles as:

$$
-5600=\underbrace{-4000}_{\text{price-only change on 20 units}}
-\underbrace{1600}_{\text{reference value of 2 units not returned}}.
$$

This 1,600 is arithmetic at our invented price and date. **It is not an automatically recognised compensation claim.** The statutory valuation rules, recovery process and eligibility can use other inputs. If the issuer's share value fell to zero, missing 2 units would have zero reference value in this calculation, but they would still be missing 2 units.

<CustodyReconciliation client:visible title="Change returned units and reference price independently" />

A higher share price would change the euro comparisons without repairing a missing-unit count. At reference price 1,200,18 returned shares are worth 21,600: above the 20,000 purchase cost, yet still 2 units short of the recorded holding, worth 2,400 at that reference price. “Overall gain” and “no custody shortfall” are not equivalent.

### When to reconcile an insolvency problem

Keep statements, trade confirmations, security identifiers, unit counts and correspondence. Check the recognised position and assets actually transferred or made available; distinguish a delayed process from a final shortfall. An issuer failure, a market sale loss and a custodian's inability to return assets can coexist, but they are different risks. Do not add the entire historical price decline to a missing-asset claim as if a compensation scheme promised the original purchase value.

## A compensation scheme has a jurisdiction, trigger, scope and limit

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="In the Spanish example, does seeing FOGAIN coverage mean a share-price loss is reimbursed up to 100,000 euros?" options={[{text:'No; it concerns eligible non-restitution under the scheme’s conditions',correct:true},{text:'Yes; the limit restores the original price of any covered shareholding'},{text:'Yes; every app account separately guarantees its first 100,000 euros'},{text:'No; client-asset return and compensation can never be relevant together'}]} explanation="FOGAIN addresses specified inability to return entrusted cash or instruments, subject to covered entity/client/services and triggering conditions. It does not insure market losses or issuer insolvency." />

For this section, assume a retail client has entrusted ordinary financial instruments to a **Spanish investment firm actually belonging to FOGAIN**. Do not infer membership from an app name, a Spanish website or a statement that the service is regulated. FOGAIN is Spain's investment-guarantee scheme for its covered entities and eligible claims; other providers and jurisdictions can have different arrangements.

As checked on 6 November 2026, FOGAIN can compensate eligible clients when a participating entity cannot return entrusted cash or financial instruments and the prescribed insolvency/non-restitution trigger is met. The required legal or supervisory event is not the same as a rumour, a temporary outage or an investment's price fall. Client, asset and activity exclusions matter. [FOGAIN explains its scope and keeps the participating-entity directory](https://www.fogain.com/inversores/), and [CNMV explains that market losses and issuer insolvency are outside this protection](https://www.cnmv.es/DocPortal/Publicaciones/Guias/Guia_50_preguntas.pdf).

### Work the aggregation and cap only after stipulating eligibility

Spanish Royal Decree 948/2001, Article 6, states a monetary limit of **100,000 euros per investor in the same entity**, aggregating the relevant accounts/positions. It contains valuation rules tied to the prescribed declarations and rules beyond our simple example. [The current consolidated text states that limit and aggregation](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2001-15221).

Suppose all eligibility and trigger conditions have been established, and the applicable process recognises an aggregate eligible unreturned monetary position of 130,000 euros for one investor against one participating firm:70,000 in one account and 60,000 in another. **Those are stipulated recognised amounts**, not claims we establish by reading a market-price screen.

$$
\text{Aggregate recognised amount}=70000+60000=130000,
$$
$$
\text{Amount within the stated cap}=\min(130000,100000)=100000,
$$
$$
\text{Amount above this cap}=130000-100000=30000.
$$

The second account does not create a second 100,000 limit. This arithmetic does not promise immediate payment, resolve exclusions, determine a legal valuation or cancel possible later recovery rights. Do not treat the entire value on a statement as money automatically due from the scheme.

### When to check protection

Identify the contracting legal entity, authorisation and actual scheme membership; read which service, client and asset types qualify. Determine who holds securities and how cash is treated. Cash in a bank deposit, client money held through a firm and a fund investment are not interchangeable legal arrangements. Banks and foreign firms may fall under other schemes; a familiar 100,000 figure alone does not identify which protection applies. Use the [FOGAIN entity directory](https://www.fogain.com/entidades-cubiertas/) and relevant official registers rather than assuming all brands, branches or affiliated companies share one coverage status.
