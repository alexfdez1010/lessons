A price leaves your liquidity range. Your position still owns tokens, but it no longer supplies active liquidity there. Moving the range can put it back to work; it can also require buying back an asset you have just sold. The decision needs an inventory budget, not a bigger-looking fee percentage.

The previous lesson compared a bounded position with full-range impermanent loss. Here we build the position itself, including asymmetric ranges, unused wallet tokens and management costs. All examples are invented, continuous **v3-style** mathematics. Actual contracts use token decimals, integer rounding and permitted ticks. A **tick** is an index on a discrete price grid; the pool’s spacing rule restricts which boundary indices a position may choose. Token decimals convert integer base units into human token amounts. We use **P = units of token B per token A**, and measure value in B. No stablecoin peg or dollar value for B is assumed.

## Define the range and the liquidity parameter

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A position has liquidity parameter L = 2,500. Does that tell you its value in dollars or its real token amounts without any prices or bounds?" options={[
 {text:'No; L must be combined with token units, range bounds and current price',correct:true},
 {text:'Yes; it holds exactly 2,500 dollars at every price'},
 {text:'Yes; it always holds 2,500 units of each token'},
]} explanation="L scales the range's inventory formulas. It is not a currency value or a fixed quantity of each token." />

**Concentrated liquidity** supplies trading capacity within selected price bounds $P_a<P_b$. The **liquidity parameter** $L$ scales that capacity. Imagine a stall agreeing to exchange stock only within a posted price interval: the interval matters as much as the stock budget. The analogy concerns an inventory rule, not a guarantee of sales or revenue.

For an inside-range price $P$, fee-free real holdings are

$$
x=L\left(\frac1{\sqrt P}-\frac1{\sqrt{P_b}}\right),\qquad
y=L\left(\sqrt P-\sqrt{P_a}\right),\qquad V^B=Px+y.
$$

$x$ is A units, $y$ is B units. $V^B$ is their value in B. The square roots are positive. Using B/A for P, L has units consistent with the square root of an A-quantity times a B-quantity; changing token units changes its numeric representation.

Take lower bound **64**, upper bound **144**, entry price **100 B/A**, and **L = 2,500**. Their square roots are 8, 12 and 10. At entry:

$$
x_0=2{,}500(1/10-1/12)=125/3\approx41.666667\text{ A},
$$

$$
y_0=2{,}500(10-8)=5{,}000\text{ B},\qquad
V_0^B=100(125/3)+5{,}000=27{,}500/3\approx9{,}166.666667\text{ B}.
$$

This deposit is not 50/50 by value: A contributes 4,166.67 B, while B contributes 5,000 B. A chosen finite range does not automatically produce the earlier lesson's equal-value full-range position.

### Real reserves and virtual reserves

Define **virtual reserves** $x_v=L/\sqrt P$ and $y_v=L\sqrt P$. They satisfy $x_vy_v=L^2$. Real amounts subtract fixed offsets:

$$
x=x_v-L/\sqrt{P_b},\qquad y=y_v-L\sqrt{P_a}.
$$

At entry, virtual A is $2{,}500/10=250$ and virtual B is $2{,}500\times10=25{,}000$. Subtract $2{,}500/12=208.333333$ A and $2{,}500\times8=20{,}000$ B to recover the real holdings. The virtual numbers are a mathematical representation of local depth, not extra tokens available for withdrawal. [Uniswap's official mathematical primer](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswap-v3-math-primer-2) explains this distinction and the piecewise holdings.

**Pitfall:** setting L to the square root of the **real** x times y. Here $x_0y_0=(125/3)\times5{,}000\approx208{,}333.333333$, while $L^2=6{,}250{,}000$. The constant-product relation applies to the virtual quantities between bounds.

### When to use these formulas

When you know a position's actual bounds, liquidity and price and want principal holdings. The result excludes separate accrued fees. For an actual deployment, read prices in the correct token order and decimal scale; a rounded displayed price alone is not the contract's exact state.

## Mint from token budgets and keep the unspent assets

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="At entry P = 100 with bounds 64/144, your wallet contains 50 A and 5,000 B. Can you deposit all of both without a swap or changing the range?" options={[
 {text:'No; the range needs about 41.666667 A for the 5,000 B, leaving about 8.333333 A outside',correct:true},
 {text:'Yes; an AMM automatically treats any quantities as the same L'},
 {text:'Yes; the unused A becomes an extra fee balance'},
]} explanation="Each side supports a different amount of L. The smaller supported amount limits the mint; the other token remains unused unless another action is explicitly performed." />

Inside the range, one unit of L needs $a=1/\sqrt P-1/\sqrt{P_b}$ A and $b=\sqrt P-\sqrt{P_a}$ B. With available amounts $X$ and $Y$:

$$
L_A=X/a,\qquad L_B=Y/b,\qquad L_{\rm mint}=\min(L_A,L_B).
$$

For our range and entry, $a=1/60$ and $b=2$. A wallet with 50 A supports $L_A=50/(1/60)=3{,}000$; its 5,000 B supports $L_B=5{,}000/2=2{,}500$. Mint L = 2,500, consume $2{,}500/60=41.666667$ A and 5,000 B, and leave $50-41.666667=8.333333$ A outside the position.

| Entry ledger, P = 100 B/A | Available | Deposited | Remains in wallet |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A units | 50 | 41.666667 | 8.333333 |
| B units | 5,000 | 5,000 | 0 |
| B value | 10,000 | 9,166.666667 | 833.333333 |

The position's starting value is 9,166.67 B, while the wallet started with 10,000 B of value. Both amounts are correct; they refer to different portfolios. At P = 121, the idle $25/3$ A is worth $(25/3)\times121=1{,}008.333333$ B. A whole-wallet comparison must include it on the strategy side. The original whole-wallet holding benchmark is the original 50 A plus 5,000 B, not just the deposited tokens.

<RangeDepositBudget client:visible />

If no B is available at this inside-range entry, B supports zero L. The model mints nothing and leaves A in the wallet; it does not borrow the missing token. A user interface may offer a swap or a combined transaction, but that is a separate priced action with its own constraints. [Uniswap's LP calculations guide](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/liquidity-providers/lp-calculations) provides the protocol calculation context.

**Pitfall:** reporting the entire wallet's fees against only the smaller deposited capital, while comparing terminal wealth against the larger wallet's starting value. Keep the same assets and scope in numerator and denominator. Real minting also has minimum-amount protections and on-chain rounding; our displayed decimals are rounded after the continuous calculation.

### When to use the budget

Before submitting a mint, adding liquidity or comparing positions. Specify whether unused tokens are kept, swapped, transferred or spent on costs. None should quietly disappear from wealth accounting.

## Follow the boundary inventory in both directions

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="With P quoted as B per A, a position moves above its upper bound. Which principal token remains?" options={[
 {text:'B, because the inventory rule has sold its A as A became more expensive',correct:true},
 {text:'A, because every rising asset is automatically accumulated'},
 {text:'Neither token; leaving a range deletes the principal'},
]} explanation="At the upper bound the A amount reaches zero. Principal stays in B above the range, with no active liquidity from this position there." />

The inside formula cannot be extended indefinitely: doing so would create negative token holdings. Define a clamped price $\widehat P=\min(P_b,\max(P_a,P))$ and use its square root for the inventory:

$$
x(P)=L\left(\frac1{\sqrt{\widehat P}}-\frac1{\sqrt{P_b}}\right),\qquad
y(P)=L\left(\sqrt{\widehat P}-\sqrt{P_a}\right).
$$

Still value the A tokens at the **actual** current P: $V^B(P)=Px(P)+y(P)$. Clamp the inventory argument, not the market value of A.

Below 64, our L = 2,500 position has $x=2{,}500(1/8-1/12)=625/6=104.166667$ A and zero B. Above 144 it has zero A and $y=2{,}500(12-8)=10{,}000$ B. The principal token amount stays fixed outside the corresponding side, but its market value need not stay fixed in your reporting currency.

| Actual P in B/A | A units | B units | Principal value in B | State |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 36 | 104.166667 | 0 | 3,750 | Below range |
| 64 | 104.166667 | 0 | 6,666.666667 | Lower boundary |
| 100 | 41.666667 | 5,000 | 9,166.666667 | Inside |
| 121 | 18.939394 | 7,500 | 9,791.666667 | Inside |
| 144 | 0 | 10,000 | 10,000 | Upper boundary |
| 169 | 0 | 10,000 | 10,000 | Above range |

At P = 36, it is essential to use $36\times104.166667=3{,}750$ B, rather than valuing at the clamped 64. Out-of-range does not freeze the token's external price. Above the upper bound, 10,000 B stays 10,000 **in B**, but B itself may change in dollars or another currency.

<ConcentratedInventoryExplorer client:visible price={169} title="Clamp the inventory, not the market price used to value it" />

The [official concentrated-liquidity explanation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/liquidity-providers/concentrated-liquidity) describes inactive liquidity outside a range. We use the continuous analogue $P_a\leq P<P_b$ for the active label: lower inclusive, upper exclusive. Actual v3 activity is determined by stored ticks and crossing state, not a rounded interface price. The token holdings are continuous at the mathematical bounds even though active status changes there.

**Pitfall:** inactive does not mean burned, liquidated or robbed of previously accrued fees. It means this principal no longer supplies active liquidity at that price. You continue to hold its remaining token and its associated risks.

### When to consider a return into range

If bounds and L stay unchanged and fees are excluded, a return to entry P = 100 restores the original 41.666667 A and 5,000 B. Inventory is a function of the current price under these assumptions. That statement does not make past fees, external token prices or management costs path-independent.

## Compare ranges with the same initial tokens

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A narrower range has twice as much L for exactly the same starting tokens. What can you conclude?" options={[
 {text:'It supplies more local depth in the overlapping interval, but not twice the wealth or guaranteed fees',correct:true},
 {text:'Its token holdings and terminal value must always be twice as large'},
 {text:'It stays active for twice as many future price paths'},
]} explanation="L measures local curve capacity conditional on the range. A narrower interval can increase L while keeping the real deposit unchanged and reducing the active price interval." />

Comparing equal L can hide unequal capital. Comparing equal starting value can hide unequal token composition. To isolate the effect of bounds, start two positions with the **same exact A and B amounts** and keep one common HODL benchmark.

Our wider range is 64 to 144 with L = 2,500. Choose the narrower range **81 to $(120/11)^2\approx119.008264$** with **L = 5,000**. Its square-root bounds are 9 and $120/11$. At P = 100:

$$
x_{0,n}=5{,}000(1/10-11/120)=125/3\text{ A},\qquad
y_{0,n}=5{,}000(10-9)=5{,}000\text{ B}.
$$

Those are the same 41.666667 A and 5,000 B. Both begin at 9,166.67 B. Doubling L did not create extra capital; it concentrated the same assets into a smaller price interval.

At P = 121 the wider position remains inside:

$$
x_w=2{,}500(1/11-1/12)=625/33\text{ A},\qquad y_w=2{,}500(11-8)=7{,}500\text{ B},
$$

$$
V_w^B=121(625/33)+7{,}500=29{,}375/3\approx9{,}791.666667\text{ B}.
$$

The narrower position is above its upper bound and holds $5{,}000(120/11-9)=105{,}000/11\approx9{,}545.454545$ B. The common holding benchmark is $(125/3)\times121+5{,}000=30{,}125/3\approx10{,}041.666667$ B.

| At the same P = 121 | Wider range | Narrower range |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| L | 2,500 | 5,000 |
| Fee-free principal in B | 9,791.666667 | 9,545.454545 |
| Common initial-token holding in B | 10,041.666667 | 10,041.666667 |
| Principal minus holding in B | −250 | −496.212121 |
| Active in the stated model | Yes | No |

<ConcentratedInventoryExplorer client:visible comparison title="Compare two ranges funded with identical initial tokens" />

For the wider range, relative underperformance is $-250/(30{,}125/3)=-6/241\approx-2.489627\%$. Its absolute principal change in B is $(29{,}375/3)/(27{,}500/3)-1\approx+6.818182\%$. A gain in B and underperformance versus holding coexist. The full-range 50/50 formula is not the valuation formula for either of these positions.

**Pitfall:** interpreting more local depth as a larger probability-weighted fee income. Fees require trading activity while active, a share of contemporaneous liquidity and the actual fee terms. A single terminal price cannot establish any of those path quantities.

### When to compare range widths

When deciding how much price movement a position can accommodate and how its inventory changes if the price leaves. Specify capital and initial tokens before comparing fee efficiency. A smaller interval is a different exposure, not an automatic improvement.

## Allocate fees along the active path

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A pool earns a net distributable LP fee pot of 1,000 B while this position is out of range. Does the position receive a share just because it owns a large L?" options={[
 {text:'No; inactive liquidity receives none of that active swap-fee allocation',correct:true},
 {text:'Yes; fees are always shared across all minted L regardless of range'},
 {text:'Yes; the protocol guarantees the same daily percentage after leaving the range'},
]} explanation="The stated fee allocation uses active liquidity at the time. Being minted is not the same as participating in every swap." />

For a simple segment with known active liquidity, the position's share is

$$
w_j=\begin{cases}L/(L+L_{{\rm other},j}),&I_j=1,\\0,&I_j=0,\end{cases}
\qquad F_j=w_jG_j.
$$

$I_j=1$ means active and $I_j=0$ inactive. $G_j$ is the **net distributable fee pot for LPs** in that segment, after any protocol allocation; it is not trading volume. $L_{{\rm other},j}$ is other active liquidity. We assume each pot is already in B and the stated shares apply throughout that segment. These invented segments illustrate allocation, not a simulated swap path or estimated income.

| Segment price B/A | Position state | Own L | Other active L | Net fee pot B | Own share | Own fees B |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 | Active | 2,500 | 7,500 | 400 | 25% | 100 |
| 169 | Inactive | 2,500 inactive | 7,500 | 1,000 | 0% | 0 |
| 121 | Active again | 2,500 | 22,500 | 200 | 10% | 20 |

Total fees are $100+0+20=120$ B. Doubling the middle fee pot leaves the position's income unchanged at 120 B. Raising final competing liquidity to 47,500 changes the last share to $2{,}500/50{,}000=5\%$ and last fees to 10 B: total 110 B. Ending inside range does not reveal which path or competing liquidity produced past fees.

<RangeFeePathLedger client:visible />

In this v3-style accounting, claimable fees are separate from principal. They do **not** automatically increase L. Reinvesting requires an actual action and appropriate token quantities. Actual swap fees can be earned in either token, so each fee balance must be valued at the chosen comparison date. [Uniswap's current fee documentation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/fees) distinguishes active fee allocation and the treatment across protocol versions; use actual pool terms rather than assuming every displayed fee rate is net LP income.

At the terminal P = 121, fee-free principal is 9,791.666667 B. Add separate fees of 120 B and subtract separate costs of 40 B once:

$$
V_{\rm net}^B=9{,}791.666667+120-40=9{,}871.666667\text{ B}.
$$

The common-date holding value is 10,041.666667 B, so the remaining gap is 170 B and the relative comparison is $-170/(30{,}125/3)\approx-1.692946\%$. For the **whole original wallet**, add idle A worth 1,008.333333 B: strategy wealth is 10,880 B versus holding $50\times121+5{,}000=11{,}050$ B. The monetary gap stays 170 B but the whole-wallet relative gap is $-170/11{,}050\approx-1.538462\%$. The denominator changed because the portfolio scope changed.

**Pitfall:** multiplying a fee rate by all pool volume and assigning the result to one position. That ignores net protocol terms, where the volume traded, active share and token of payment. An advertised annualized rate also needs a horizon and reinvestment convention; this ledger has no APR or forecast probability.

### When to reconstruct fee income

When assessing an actual position, retrieve claimable/collected balances and liquidity history with consistent block/date boundaries. Count a fee already embedded in the reported wealth once. Use hypothetical activity assumptions explicitly when stress testing future income.

## Recenter with a complete self-financing budget

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="The original position is above its upper bound and holds 10,000 B. You move to a new range requiring both tokens at the current price. What funds the new A amount if no outside cash is added?" options={[
 {text:'Some withdrawn B must buy A, and all extra costs reduce the amount available for new assets',correct:true},
 {text:'L automatically creates the missing A without spending B'},
 {text:'Changing the displayed bounds converts the old holding benchmark into zero'},
]} explanation="A new range can require a different mix. The token trade and transaction costs must fit the assets withdrawn or another explicitly declared contribution." />

At P = 169, the original 64/144 position holds **10,000 B**. Compare leaving it alone with one explicit management action: withdraw, spend **40 B total extra execution/transaction cost**, buy A at the **assumed execution price of 169 B/A**, and mint a new continuous range **121 to 225**. No fees or external cash are added in this separate example. The 40 B is charged once; price impact and gas are represented only by that declared all-in allowance, not another hidden deduction.

After cost, 9,960 B remains for assets. The new square-root bounds are 11 and 15, with current square root 13. Per unit of new L:

$$
x/L=1/13-1/15=2/195,\qquad y/L=13-11=2,
$$

$$
V^B/L=169(2/195)+2=56/15,\qquad
L_{\rm new}=9{,}960/(56/15)=18{,}675/7\approx2{,}667.857143.
$$

The new A amount is $L_{\rm new}(2/195)=2{,}490/91\approx27.362637$ A. Buying it costs $169\times(2{,}490/91)=4{,}624.285714$ B. The new B inventory is $2L_{\rm new}=5{,}335.714286$ B. The complete budget is

$$
4{,}624.285714+5{,}335.714286+40=10{,}000\text{ B}.
$$

New L is larger than old L = 2,500, but the new assets are worth only 9,960 B immediately after the action. A larger liquidity parameter has not refunded the cost.

<RangeRecenterComparison client:visible />

Now follow one **invented reversal** to P = 100. The new position is below 121, holding $L_{\rm new}(1/11-1/15)\approx64.675325$ A and zero B, worth **6,467.532468 B**. Leaving the original range unchanged would have restored **9,166.666667 B** at that same P. Original-token HODL is also 9,166.666667 B. This example shows a management-path consequence, not a prediction that recentering always loses. Another path can favor the changed range, and actual fees/costs need their own ledger.

The original HODL benchmark stays the original 41.666667 A plus 5,000 B. Creating a new position does not erase the relative gap that existed at the recenter date. Report external additions or withdrawals explicitly rather than presenting them as investment performance.

**Pitfall:** treating a crossed range as an irrevocable limit order. If you leave the position intact and price returns, trading can reverse its token conversion. [Uniswap's range-order explanation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/liquidity-providers/range-orders) describes why actual withdrawal/management matters. A range and a one-shot order are different execution rules.

### When to leave, change or withdraw a range

Compare a dated management policy, available tokens, actual execution quotes, transaction costs, possible fee income and adverse paths. Include token/depeg, contract, oracle/interface and access risks. Concentrating capital makes a specific inventory and activity choice; it does not supply a universally suitable range or guaranteed yield.
