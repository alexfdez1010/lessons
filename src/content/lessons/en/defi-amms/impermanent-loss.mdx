A position starts at **10,000 dollars** and later is worth **14,142.14 dollars**. It gained value. The exact tokens originally deposited would now be worth **15,000 dollars** if left untouched. It also underperformed that holding alternative.

Both statements can be true. **Impermanent loss**, or **IL**, describes the relative comparison, rather than every possible meaning of “loss”. We will calculate the inventory, state the currency and benchmark, and then put fees and costs on matching denominators. The arithmetic below is an ideal model, not a forecast of a deployed pool.

## 1. Specify what you are comparing, in which currency and at which date

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A fee-free LP position grows from 10,000 to 14,142.14 dollars while holding the original tokens would be worth 15,000. What follows?" options={[
 {text:'The LP gained about 41.42% in dollars but underperformed holding by about 5.72%',correct:true},
 {text:'The LP lost 5.72% of the initial 10,000 dollars'},
 {text:'The LP must have been charged a separate 857.86-dollar protocol fee'},
]} explanation="The absolute change uses the initial value as denominator. The relative comparison uses the final holding value. The difference is not a separate fee invoice." />

A **liquidity provider**, or **LP**, supplies assets to a trading pool. **HODL** here means keeping the **exact initial token quantities**, with no trades, additions or withdrawals. It does not mean buying only the token that later performs best.

Let $V_H$ be the terminal value of those original tokens and $V_L^{(0)}$ the terminal value of the fee-free LP reference. “Terminal” means the same chosen comparison date. In a common reporting currency, define

$$
IL^{(0)}=\frac{V_L^{(0)}}{V_H}-1.
$$

The superscript zero reminds us that fees and costs are excluded. The monetary shortfall is $V_H-V_L^{(0)}$, while absolute LP change is $V_L^{(0)}/V_0-1$, with $V_0$ the starting value. They use different denominators.

For our opening numbers, the gap is $15{,}000-14{,}142.1356=857.8644$ dollars. Relative IL is $14{,}142.1356/15{,}000-1=-5.7191\%$. Absolute LP change is $14{,}142.1356/10{,}000-1=+41.4214\%$.

The [official Uniswap v2 return explanation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v2/concepts/understanding-returns) uses the unchanged initial-token holding benchmark. Its assumptions matter; this definition is not a promise that all actual LP positions must trail holding after fees.

### When to use this comparison

Before comparing two dashboard percentages, write their start date, end date, currency, benchmark quantities and treatment of fees. “Up 40%” and “behind holding by 6%” can describe the same position. Neither number alone describes all investment risks.

## 2. Derive the 50/50 fee-free result from the inventory

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="In a fee-free A/B constant-product pool, A’s price in B doubles. What happens to the LP's A inventory after ideal arbitrage?" options={[
 {text:'It falls; B inventory rises',correct:true},
 {text:'Both token quantities remain unchanged'},
 {text:'A inventory must double because its price doubled'},
]} explanation="Arbitrage buys the underpriced A from the pool and supplies B until the reserve ratio matches the new relative price in the model." />

A **constant-product pool** has token quantities $x$ of A and $y$ of B, with $xy=k$. Its marginal price of A in B is $P=y/x$. **Arbitrage** trades against a price difference; here we assume it brings the pool to the external relative price instantly and without a fee band.

Use a **50/50 starting allocation by value**, full-range liquidity, a fixed proportional claim on the model inventory, standard non-rebasing tokens and no fees, costs or liquidity changes. “50/50” does not mean equal token counts. If A initially costs $P_0=2{,}000$ B and B initially equals one reporting dollar, depositing 10,000 B of value gives

$$
x_0=\frac{5{,}000}{2{,}000}=2.5\text{ A},\qquad y_0=5{,}000\text{ B},\qquad k=12{,}500.
$$

Define the **relative-price ratio** $r=P_1/P_0>0$. Solving $x_1y_1=k$ and $y_1/x_1=P_1$ gives

$$
x_1=\frac{x_0}{\sqrt r},\qquad y_1=y_0\sqrt r.
$$

At $r=2$, $P_1=4{,}000$ B per A. Displayed decimals are rounded; the calculation retains unrounded quantities:

| Inventory | HODL | Fee-free LP |
|---|---:|---:|
| A quantity | 2.5 | $2.5/\sqrt2\approx1.76776695$ |
| B quantity | 5,000 | $5{,}000\sqrt2\approx7{,}071.06781$ |
| Value in B | $2.5(4{,}000)+5{,}000=15{,}000$ | $1.76776695(4{,}000)+7{,}071.06781\approx14{,}142.13562$ |

Using the initial quote-token value $V_0^B$, holding gives $V_H^B=V_0^B(1+r)/2$ and the fee-free LP gives $V_L^{B,(0)}=V_0^B\sqrt r$. Divide the two:

$$
IL^{(0)}(r)=\frac{2\sqrt r}{1+r}-1.
$$

<ImpermanentLossCurve client:visible showFees={false} title="Follow the inventories and the holding benchmark" />

The result is zero at $r=1$ and nonpositive for every positive $r$, because $1+r\geq2\sqrt r$. It is symmetric under $r\mapsto1/r$. A doubling and a halving both give about −5.7191% **relative to their respective HODL values**, not the same monetary shortfall.

| r | IL | Fee-free LP value in B, starting at 10,000 | HODL value in B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 | −5.7191% | 7,071.07 | 7,500 |
| 1 | 0% | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| 1.25 | −0.6192% | 11,180.34 | 11,250 |
| 2 | −5.7191% | 14,142.14 | 15,000 |
| 4 | −20% | 20,000 | 25,000 |

### When the formula applies

Use it as a clean full-range benchmark under these assumptions. Fees change the reserve product, real arbitrage has costs, and a token that rebases or charges transfer fees may break the simple inventory model. Do not replace “fee-free 50/50 full-range comparison” with “the return of every AMM”.

]} explanation="20,000/25,000 − 1 = −20%. Separately, 20,000/10,000 − 1 = +100%. The 5,000 B gap is 20% of HODL, not 20% of the deposit." />

## 3. The quote token is a unit, not a guaranteed dollar

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A’s price measured in B doubles, while B's dollar value halves. Did A's dollar price necessarily double too?" options={[
 {text:'No. Multiplying the two changes leaves A’s dollar price unchanged',correct:true},
 {text:'Yes. A/B and A/USD are the same price'},
 {text:'No. B can never change its dollar value'},
]} explanation="A/USD = (A quoted in B) × (B/USD). A relative-price ratio of two and a quote-token dollar ratio of one half multiply to one." />

A **reporting currency**, sometimes called a **numeraire**, is the unit in which values are compared. Our formula uses A priced in B. It does not require B to be a risk-free dollar. To convert both final portfolios to dollars, multiply both B values by the same final dollar price of B.

With $r=2$ and B still worth one dollar, HODL is 15,000 dollars and the LP 14,142.14 dollars. If B instead falls from one dollar to **0.50 dollars**, with the same $r=2$:

$$
V_H^{\mathrm{USD}}=15{,}000(0.50)=7{,}500,\qquad V_L^{\mathrm{USD},(0)}=14{,}142.1356(0.50)=7{,}071.0678.
$$

Relative IL stays $7{,}071.0678/7{,}500-1=-5.7191\%$, because the common conversion cancels. But absolute LP change from the initial 10,000 dollars is now $7{,}071.0678/10{,}000-1=-29.2893\%$, rather than +41.4214%. A's dollar price is $4{,}000(0.50)=2{,}000$, unchanged from entry.

<ImpermanentLossCurve client:visible showFees={false} showQuoteValue title="Change B’s dollar value without changing the relative-price formula" />

### When to use a currency check

Use a common terminal price for each token in both alternatives, including any depegged asset. A **depeg** is a departure from an intended price link. If two tokens both halve in dollars while their relative price stays unchanged, $r=1$ and model IL is zero, but both portfolios can still lose half their dollar value. Small relative divergence does not establish safety. The percentage requires a positive final holding benchmark; if that value is zero, the ratio is undefined. This model also does not resolve an inability to value or withdraw a token or pool position.

## 4. The word “impermanent” does not postpone the economic cost

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="The unchanged-token benchmark is worth 15,000 dollars today and your fee-free LP 14,142.14. Does the gap exist before removing liquidity?" options={[
 {text:'Yes. The economic comparison can be measured today',correct:true},
 {text:'No. Removing liquidity creates the entire historical gap'},
 {text:'No. The protocol owes the gap back after a fixed waiting period'},
]} explanation="The positions already have different inventories and values. Withdrawal changes custody or form; it is not what creates the measured underperformance." />

The name comes from an endpoint property of the ideal model. If the relative price returns to $r=1$, with the same fee-free invariant and no liquidity changes, $x_1=x_0$ and $y_1=y_0$. The model's relative gap becomes zero. It is **not guaranteed** that prices return, or that every real cost disappears if they do.

Trace a model round trip with B at one dollar:

| Relative price r | Fee-free LP value | HODL value | Monetary gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Entry, 1 | 10,000 | 10,000 | 0 |
| Intermediate, 2 | 14,142.14 | 15,000 | 857.86 |
| Return to entry, 1 | 10,000 | 10,000 | 0 |

The intermediate gap is observable even if liquidity remains in the pool. A separately paid 100-dollar transaction cost would still leave wealth 100 dollars lower after this price round trip, absent an offsetting receipt. A return of the token ratio does not refund it.

### When to use the name carefully

“Realised” can refer to different accounting, tax or trading conventions. For this lesson, compare economic values on a date and state what was sold or withdrawn. Removing liquidity returns the current inventory; keeping those tokens afterward still leaves future price exposure. Neither waiting nor withdrawal guarantees recovery. **Divergence loss** is a useful alternative name, provided its benchmark and model remain explicit.

## 5. Put fees and costs on a common monetary base

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="The no-fee LP shortfall is 857.86 dollars. A separately specified terminal fee credit equals 8% of the initial 10,000 dollars, or 800. Does it cover the shortfall before costs?" options={[
 {text:'No. 800 is less than 857.86',correct:true},
 {text:'Yes. 8 is greater than 5.72, so the percentages can always be added'},
 {text:'Yes. Fees and divergence loss always cancel'},
]} explanation="Eight percent of the deposit and 5.72% of terminal HODL have different denominators. Convert them to amounts or use one shared base." />

For an **illustrative accounting bridge**, add a terminal fee credit $F$ valued in the same reporting currency to the fee-free reference and subtract separately specified terminal costs $C$:

$$
\frac{V_L^{(0)}+F-C}{V_H}-1=IL^{(0)}+\frac{F-C}{V_H}.
$$

This equality is exact for that bridge. It is not a simulation of a fee-charging pool's trades or compounding. With $V_L^{(0)}=14{,}142.1356$, $V_H=15{,}000$, $F=800$ and $C=0$, the combined value is 14,942.1356 dollars and the net comparison is $14{,}942.1356/15{,}000-1=-0.3858\%$.

| What “8% fees” means in the toy bridge | Terminal credit | Net versus HODL before separate costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 8% of initial 10,000 | 800.00 | −0.3858% |
| 8% of fee-free LP 14,142.14 | 1,131.37 | +1.8234% |
| 8% of HODL 15,000 | 1,200.00 | +2.2809% |

Only the last row permits directly adding $8\%+(-5.7191\%)$. If costs are 100 dollars, the first row falls to $14{,}842.1356/15{,}000-1=-1.0524\%$. Its break-even fee credit is the monetary gap plus costs, $857.8644+100=957.8644$ dollars.

### Actual position accounting needs more than an endpoint formula

For a real comparison, value the actual remaining token inventory, unclaimed fees, claimed fees still held and any external receipts at the same date, then subtract costs consistently. **Do not add fees again if the reported position value already includes them.** If tokens were added or removed, compare a benchmark with matching external cash flows instead of using the original one-deposit formula blindly.

[Uniswap's current fee documentation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/fees) distinguishes v2 fees added to reserves from separately claimable fees in v3/v4, as well as protocol shares. Fee income depends on the trades, active liquidity and rules actually applying to the position. An annualised fee rate is not a guaranteed terminal cash amount; volume alone does not establish net profitability. Gas, rebalancing costs, rewards changing value and contract risk may also matter.

## 6. Full-range V2 maths is not a universal range-position formula

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A concentrated-liquidity position leaves its selected price range. Can you use the full-range 50/50 formula as its exact inventory valuation without its bounds?" options={[
 {text:'No. The bounds and initial position determine the inventory',correct:true},
 {text:'Yes. Every AMM position is always 50/50 by value'},
 {text:'Yes. Out-of-range liquidity keeps earning the same fees automatically'},
]} explanation="A bounded position can become entirely one token and stop providing active liquidity. The full-range reference is a different position." />

**Concentrated liquidity** allocates liquidity within selected price bounds. In the ideal continuous mathematics, let the entry relative price be 1 and choose reciprocal bounds $l=0.5$ and $u=2$. Start with the same equal-value inventory: $a_0=5{,}000$ in A measured at its entry price, and $b_0=5{,}000$ B. Here $a$ is A quantity multiplied by its entry B price; actual A units are $a/P_0$.

Define $\ell=5{,}000/(1-1/\sqrt2)=17{,}071.06781$. For relative prices inside the interval, the normalized amounts are

$$
a(r)=\ell\left(\frac1{\sqrt r}-\frac1{\sqrt u}\right),\qquad b(r)=\ell(\sqrt r-\sqrt l),\qquad V^B(r)=a(r)r+b(r).
$$

At $r=1$, both amounts are 5,000. At the upper bound $r=2$, $a=0$ and

$$
b=17{,}071.06781(\sqrt2-\sqrt{0.5})=12{,}071.06781.
$$

With B at one dollar, this bounded position is worth 12,071.07 dollars versus HODL 15,000: $12{,}071.06781/15{,}000-1=-19.5262\%$. The full-range no-fee position at the same price is worth 14,142.14, with −5.7191% IL. The positions started with the same assets but followed different inventory rules.

Above the upper bound the principal stays entirely in B until the relative price returns to the range or the position changes. At $r=4$, it is still 12,071.07 B before fees, while HODL is 25,000 B: relative underperformance is about −51.7157%. This does not apply the inside-range formula to negative token quantities; inventory is fixed at the boundary outside it.

[Uniswap's concentrated-liquidity documentation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/liquidity-providers/concentrated-liquidity) explains inactive, single-token positions outside their range. Its [official mathematical primer](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswap-v3-math-primer-2) details inventory and uncollected-fee calculations. Our continuous illustration omits tick rounding, fee paths and contract-specific implementation details.

### When a small relative gap still leaves substantial risk

A narrow range changes exposure and can increase fee-free underperformance relative to this holding benchmark. It does not guarantee higher realised fees. Stablecoin labels also cannot guarantee a small relative move: a depeg can move the ratio outside the assumed range. Both token prices falling together can create absolute losses with zero model IL. Inspect range, token, contract, access and currency risks alongside the benchmark comparison.
