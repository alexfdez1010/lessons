A country imports a machine worth 100 and borrows 100 from abroad to pay for it. Two headlines appear: “Imports increased” and “Foreign financing increased.” They describe two sides of the same transaction. Adding them as though they were two independent gains would count the machine twice.

The **balance of payments**, or **BOP**, records transactions between an economy's residents and nonresidents over a period. It helps answer two different questions: *what was exchanged or transferred?* and *which financial claims changed?* The [IMF's BOP dataset](https://data.imf.org/en/datasets/IMF.STA:BOP) covers these external accounts. Our numbers are invented, in millions of one common currency, for one year unless stated otherwise.

<Callout variant="info" title="Check the manual and the signs before comparing tables">
The IMF released BPM7, its updated external-accounts manual, in March 2025, with a recommended implementation window of 2029–2030. Published series can still use BPM6. BPM7 calls primary income **earned income**, secondary income **transfer income**, and net errors and omissions the **statistical discrepancy**. We give both familiar names and use the asset-minus-liability financial-account convention throughout. A table using “net capital inflow” may reverse that last sign. [IMF release and implementation timetable](https://www.imf.org/en/News/Articles/2025/03/20/pr25072-imf-and-statistical-community-release-new-global-standards-for-macroeconomic-stats).
</Callout>

## 1. The boundary is residence, and each transaction has two entries

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="An exporter delivers goods worth 100 today and lets the overseas customer pay next month. Is today's balance of payments empty?" options={[
  { text: "No. Record an export and an external trade-credit asset of 100", correct: true },
  { text: "Yes. Only physical bank transfers count" },
  { text: "Record two exports, because payment is delayed" },
]} explanation="Delivery transfers economic ownership in this simplified example. The seller also acquires a claim on the nonresident customer. Cash settlement can occur later." />

**Residence** concerns where a person or organisation has its predominant economic interest, rather than passport, ownership nationality or payment currency. A locally resident company can have foreign owners. For this lesson, all transactions are clearly between a resident and a nonresident; the manual has special rules for borderline cases.

**Double entry** means recording both sides of a transaction at the same value. It resembles keeping the receipt and the explanation of how you paid, with one important extension: payment need not be immediate cash. The [ECB's official methods guide](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pubbydate/2025/html/ecb.bopiipbook202507.en.html) explains this ownership and transaction approach.

A **deposit** is a claim on a bank; a **bond** is a tradable debt claim. **Trade credit** is a claim created when a sale is paid later.

| Separate invented transaction | First entry | Matching entry |
|---|---|---|
| Export goods for 100, paid into a resident's foreign-bank deposit | Goods export +100 | External deposit asset acquisition +100 |
| Export goods for 100, payment due next month | Goods export +100 | Trade-credit asset acquisition +100 |
| Buy a foreign bond for 100 using an existing foreign deposit | Bond asset acquisition +100 | Deposit asset disposal −100 |

The third transaction has no export and no current-account income merely because a bond was bought. Its two financial-asset changes net to zero: $100 + (-100) = 0$. Buying a financial claim and earning income on it are different events.

### When to use this boundary

Before classifying a headline, identify the resident unit, the counterparty, the period and the economic event. A euro payment between two domestic residents is generally outside this external ledger. A transaction with a nonresident can be inside it without any currency conversion.

## 2. The current account is wider than the trade balance

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="Goods and services exports equal imports. Must the current account be zero?" options={[
  { text: "No. Cross-border income and current transfers can change the balance", correct: true },
  { text: "Yes. The current account is just exports minus imports" },
  { text: "No. Every foreign share purchase belongs in the current account" },
]} explanation="Trade is one component. Income earned across borders and current transfers also belong in the current account; buying shares is a financial transaction." />

The **current account**, **CA**, combines trade in goods and services with earned income and current transfers. **Earned/primary income** includes investment income and qualifying employee compensation; **transfer/secondary income** includes current transfers with no equivalent resource received in return, such as qualifying household transfers. A transfer's precise classification depends on its nature, rather than whether it arrives in a bank account. The [OECD's official BPM7-aligned explanation](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-benchmark-definition-of-foreign-direct-investment-fifth-edition_7f05c0a3-en/full-report/links-between-fdi-and-other-statistics_e6f184d4.html) sets out this current-account decomposition.

$$
CA = (X-M) + \text{net earned income} + \text{net current transfers}
$$

$X$ means exports, $M$ imports. In the current account, a **credit/revenue** contributes positively and a **debit/expenditure** negatively. “Credit” here does not mean taking out a loan.

| Component | Credits | Debits | Balance: credits − debits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Goods | 80 | 110 | −30 |
| Services | 40 | 40 | 0 |
| Earned income | 8 | 12 | −4 |
| Current transfers | 6 | 2 | +4 |
| Current account | 134 | 164 | −30 |

Exports are $80+40=120$ and imports $110+40=150$. Thus trade is $120-150=-30$. Add income and transfers: $-30+(8-12)+(6-2)=-30-4+4=-30$.

<BopAccountBridge client:visible view="current" title="Build the current account from its components" />

Move net earned income from −4 to +6. The current account improves by 10 to −20, although the trade balance stays at −30. “Current-account deficit” and “trade deficit” happen to match in the original example because the other balances offset; they are not synonyms.

### When to use it

Use the decomposition to ask whether a change came from exports, imports, income payments or transfers. A deficit is a signed flow for the period, not a verdict that every resident lost money or that all imports were wasteful.

<MCQ client:visible question="Exports are 90, imports 100, net earned income +7 and net current transfers +5. Calculate CA." options={[
  { text: "+2", correct: true },
  { text: "−10" },
  { text: "+12" },
  { text: "−22" },
]} explanation="CA = 90 − 100 + 7 + 5 = −10 + 12 = +2. The trade deficit coexists with a current-account surplus." />

## 3. The capital account is a specific account, not all investment

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="A nonresident buys newly issued shares in a resident company for 20. Which account records that financial claim?" options={[
  { text: "The financial account", correct: true },
  { text: "The capital account, because shares are called capital" },
  { text: "The goods account, because a company was bought" },
]} explanation="Everyday uses of capital are broader than the statistical capital account. A share transaction creates or transfers a financial claim." />

The **capital account**, **KA**, covers capital transfers and transactions in nonproduced, nonfinancial assets. **Capital transfers** include qualifying investment grants and agreed debt forgiveness. **Nonproduced, nonfinancial assets** are a defined statistical category, including certain transferable contracts and marketing assets; the label does not mean every asset used by a business. The [IMF manual, chapter 14 and its debt-forgiveness annex](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/data/statistics/bpm6/draft-bpm7-wcv.pdf), distinguishes these items from financial investment.

Here is a clean no-cash example. A nonresident official creditor and resident debtor agree to forgive an existing debt worth 2. Assume no other entries or accrued interest in this isolated example.

| Debtor economy entry | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Capital transfer received, KA | +2 |
| Net incurrence of external liabilities, L | −2 |
| Net acquisition of external assets, A | 0 |
| Financial balance, A − L | $0-(-2)=+2$ |

No new cash arrived. Both measures of net lending still rise by 2: $CA+KA=0+2=2$ and $FA=0-(-2)=2$. Removing a liability is not the same event as borrowing more.

<Reveal client:visible>
A creditor unilaterally writes off an uncollectable debt, without a forgiveness agreement. Does that automatically create the same capital transfer? No. A write-off is generally an **other volume change**, outside the transaction accounts. The agreement and economic event determine the treatment. Our next example uses an agreed transfer, not a write-off disguised as one.
</Reveal>

### When to use the distinction

When a headline says “capital entered the country”, find out whether it means foreign financial investment, a capital transfer or an informal aggregate. Those labels can point to different accounts. A country's domestic factory construction is also not automatically an external capital-account transaction.

## 4. The financial account: assets minus liabilities

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="Residents acquire 12 of external assets and incur 40 of external liabilities. Under FA = A − L, what is the balance?" options={[
  { text: "−28: net borrowing in the statistical sense", correct: true },
  { text: "+28: the same sign as net inward financing" },
  { text: "+52: add the two sides" },
]} explanation="A − L = 12 − 40 = −28. Net inward financing uses the opposite sign, L − A = +28." />

**External financial assets** include residents' financial claims on nonresidents, as in our examples; monetary gold is a special asset without a corresponding liability. An **external liability** is a nonresident's claim on a resident. Both include equity as well as debt, so statistical **net borrowing** does not mean every financing transaction is a loan.

Let **A** be net acquisitions less disposals (assets sold or otherwise relinquished) of external financial assets, and **L** be net incurrences less repayments or redemptions of external liabilities. Each can be negative. Then:

$$
FA = A-L
$$

Positive $FA$ means **net lending**; negative $FA$ means **net borrowing**. This is the convention in the [Bank of Japan's official BOP explanation](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/statistics/outline/exp/faqbpsm6.htm). A plus sign on $L$ means liabilities increased, not that the financial-account balance increased.

<BopAccountBridge client:visible view="financial" title="A positive liability increase is subtracted" />

Our full example has $CA=-30$ and agreed capital transfers giving $KA=+2$. Their sum is $-28$. If the observations agree, residents can acquire $A=12$ while incurring $L=40$: $12-40=-28$. It would be wrong to say that financing was only 28 on the liability side: 40 entered that side while residents also acquired 12 of assets.

Now add 100 to both sides: $A=112$, $L=140$, and $FA=112-140=-28$ again. The same net balance can hide much larger activity. Published “net acquisitions” and “net incurrences” are themselves netted within each side; they are not necessarily complete gross turnover.

The financial account groups **direct investment** (an investment relationship involving control or significant influence), **portfolio investment** (securities outside that relationship), **financial derivatives** (contracts linked to a reference), **other investment** (including many loans and deposits) and reserve assets. These are broad guides; official classification rules settle the boundaries. **Official reserve assets** are external assets controlled by monetary authorities under the applicable definition; reserve transactions belong inside the financial account. Adding them again as a separate fourth account double counts them. Acquiring foreign shares is a transaction; a later price rise in the same holding is not an acquisition.

### When to use the signs

Translate an “inflow” headline into a named variable before interpreting it. $L-A=28$ is positive net inward financing; the same observations produce $FA=-28$. Neither convention predicts the exchange rate. Currency conversion, offsetting transactions and policy reactions need their own analysis.

## 5. Statistical discrepancies expose a measurement gap

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="CA + KA is −28, while independently recorded FA is −26. Should a compiler silently change imports by 2?" options={[
  { text: "No. Show the residual explicitly and investigate the source data", correct: true },
  { text: "Yes. An identity proves which observation is wrong" },
  { text: "No. The gap proves hidden illegal inflows of exactly 2" },
]} explanation="The residual shows disagreement between measures. It does not identify the responsible component or a unique economic cause." />

In a complete, consistently measured ledger, $CA+KA=FA$. Real statistics combine surveys, administrative records and other sources with different coverage and timing. Define the **statistical discrepancy**, **E**, also known as **net errors and omissions**, in this lesson as:

$$
E = FA-(CA+KA), \qquad CA+KA+E=FA
$$

Suppose observed $A=12$ and $L=38$, so $FA=-26$, while $CA+KA=-28$. Then $E=-26-(-28)=+2$, and $-30+2+2=-26$. A positive residual can reflect an understated current credit, an overstated debit, an overstated asset acquisition or an understated liability increase. It does not tell us which one occurred.

<BopAccountBridge client:visible liabilities={38} title="Keep independent observations and reveal the residual" />

First move liabilities from 38 to 40: the residual falls from +2 to zero. Return to 38, then raise exports from 120 to 122 instead: the residual also becomes zero. These are **alternative arithmetic reconciliations**, not evidence that either correction is true.

Errors can also cancel. If one unrecorded current credit of 5 and one unrecorded current debit of 5 offset, their net effect on the residual is zero. A zero discrepancy is therefore not a certificate of perfect data. The [IMF manual's discussion of the statistical discrepancy, chapter 2](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/data/statistics/bpm6/draft-bpm7-wcv.pdf), makes that limitation explicit.

### When to use it

Read discrepancies, revisions and methodology notes before making strong claims from small balance changes. Compare the same periods and units. A monthly flow in millions cannot be reconciled directly with a year-end stock in billions.

<MCQ client:visible question="CA = +6, KA = −1 and FA = +3. Under our stated convention, which residual closes the identity?" options={[
  { text: "E = −2", correct: true },
  { text: "E = +2" },
  { text: "E = +8" },
  { text: "E = 0, because accounts always match in observed data" },
]} explanation="CA + KA = 5. E = FA − (CA + KA) = 3 − 5 = −2. Check: 6 − 1 − 2 = 3." />

## 6. Flows change positions, but prices change them too

<MCQ client:visible pretest question="The net external financial position worsened by 20. Must this year's financial-account balance be −20?" options={[
  { text: "No. Valuation effects and other volume changes can also alter the position", correct: true },
  { text: "Yes. A closing stock is always equal to one year's flow" },
  { text: "No. Financial transactions never affect external positions" },
]} explanation="A stock is measured at a date. The financial account is one source of its change during a period, alongside valuation and other changes." />

The **international investment position**, **IIP**, is the stock of external financial assets and liabilities at a date. **Net IIP** is assets minus liabilities. It is neither national wealth nor external debt alone: it includes financial equity claims and omits domestic nonfinancial assets.

In a consistent common-currency net-position bridge:

$$
\text{closing net IIP}=\text{opening net IIP}+FA+\text{valuation changes}+\text{other volume changes}
$$

Start at −100. Transactions contribute −28, net price and exchange-rate effects +10, and other volume changes −2. The closing position is $-100-28+10-2=-120$. It deteriorated by 20, although the transaction flow was −28. The [ECB's external-accounts methods guide](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pubbydate/2025/html/ecb.bopiipbook202507.en.html) distinguishes revaluations from transactions.

<ExternalPositionBridge client:visible />

Change valuation effects from +10 to +30: $-100-28+30-2=-100$. The position is unchanged even with net borrowing transactions of 28. Conversely, asset-price losses can worsen a position without any new borrowing transaction. The composition and currency of both sides matter.

### What the identity can and cannot tell you

The accounts constrain a consistent explanation; they do not establish a causal direction. “A current-account deficit made foreigners invest” and “foreign financing caused the deficit” each need an economic mechanism and evidence. The same $CA+KA=-28$ can accompany long-term equity investment, short-term debt or asset disposals. Their refinancing, currency and income risks differ.

Use balances to start a diagnosis: which sector, instrument, maturity, currency, counterparty and valuation effect changed? Avoid treating a surplus as automatically healthy or a deficit as automatically dangerous. A signed total is the first page of the investigation.
