## Cases for the timing questions

All amounts are invented dollars and all dates are elapsed end-of-day dates in a 30-day period. Contributions are positive from the portfolio viewpoint; withdrawals negative. Opening values exclude separately listed start-boundary flows, ending values include final-boundary flows. No fees or taxes.

**Dietz:** begin 100, end 130, contribution 40 at day 6, withdrawal 20 at day 24. Each candidate gives gain / weighted capital / period return.

**Modest:** begin 100, midpoint contribution 10, end 120. Dietz is 9.523810%; the exact compounded cash-flow IRR is 9.534144%, both for the period. The portfolio value immediately before the contribution is unknown.

**Exit:** begin 100, grow to 300 by day 15, withdraw 300 then, leave zero and end zero. The weighted-capital calculation uses the whole 30-day period.

**Extreme:** begin 100; first-half return +100% gives 200; midpoint contribution 1,000 gives 1,200; second-half return −50% leaves 600. Exact TWR 0%, Dietz −83.333333%, exact period IRR −67.764363%. These are three results on the same 30-day basis.

## Key Takeaways

<Callout variant="success" title="You passed if…">
- You read **ROI and CAGR** as different jobs — total gain vs. the geometric yearly rate — and never let an arithmetic average disguise the volatility drag.
- You treat **volatility and drawdown** as complementary, and you instinctively reach for `1/(1 − d) − 1` when someone shrugs off a deep fall.
- You keep **Sharpe, Sortino and Calmar** definitions explicit: arithmetic mean excess return for Sharpe, arithmetic mean minus the same downside target for Sortino, and CAGR over historical drawdown for Calmar. Match observation and denominator conventions; a zero denominator gives no finite ratio.
- You distinguish **exposure from adjusted performance**: beta is fitted sensitivity and alpha is a model-relative intercept. Check uncertainty and other exposures before making claims about skill.
- You separate **contributions from gains**, use **TWR** for the linked investment path and **MWR** for the investor’s dated cash flows, and compare matching time and fee bases.
- **Modified Dietz** estimates a period return from bookends and weighted external flows; retain the daily convention, approximation limits and denominator checks.
- Read return, risk, exposure and cash-flow measures **together**, matching their periods and assumptions; the metrics describe performance without proving its cause.
</Callout>
