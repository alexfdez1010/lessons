The network reduced forecast error. The trading policy lost money. Both statements can be correct. We need to examine what was predicted, which information was available, how the model was selected and what trading those predictions actually cost.

The architecture lessons gave us recurrent networks, temporal convolutions, attention and embeddings. This lesson evaluates the **whole procedure**: data versions, preprocessing, training, hyperparameter and seed selection, forecasts, positions and execution. A particular model family, regulariser or cross-validation name does not make that procedure honest by itself.

Every numerical population, forecast record and trading fee below is invented. We show the assumptions needed for each calculation rather than treating them as facts about all financial datasets.

## 1. Information depends on the statistic and the dependence

<MCQ client:visible pretest
 question={"A panel grows from one name to twenty per date. Must its independent information grow 20-fold?"}
 options={[{"text": "No; derive precision for a specified statistic and dependence model", "correct": true}, {"text": "Yes, because every row is a separate independent experiment"}, {"text": "No; names beyond the first can never add any information"}, {"text": "Yes; a large dataset proves stationary trading performance"}]}
 explanation={"Shared date shocks can remain as idiosyncratic noise averages away. More information is possible; a universal row multiplier is not established."}

Photographing the same event from twenty seats gives twenty views, not twenty independent events. But some information in each view can still be new. A useful information calculation must specify which quantity we estimate.

Suppose we estimate a **population mean** from $D$ independent dates and $M$ names per date. At each date,

$$
Y_{di}=Z_d+\varepsilon_{di},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_d)=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_{di})=1.
$$

All shocks are centred; different dates are independent; idiosyncratic shocks are independent of one another and of every date shock. Two names on the same date share $Z_d$. Each individual observation therefore has variance $1+1=2$ in outcome units squared.

The average is $\bar Y=D^{-1}\sum_dZ_d+(DM)^{-1}\sum_{d,i}\varepsilon_{di}$, so

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y)=\frac1D+\frac1{DM}.
$$

For $D=100$ and $M=20$, 2,000 rows give mean variance $0.01+0.0005=0.0105$. Incorrect IID treatment would give $2/2000=0.001$. Define an **IID-equivalent sample size for this mean** by matching variance to $2/N_{\rm eq}$:

$$
N_{\rm eq}=\frac2{0.0105}=190.476190\ldots.
$$

<TemporalModelAudit client:visible mode="information" title="More names do not remove a common date shock" caption="Invented sample-mean model: 100 independent dates, one shared unit-variance shock per date and independent unit-variance name shocks. Increase names while the number of independent dates stays fixed." />

At one name per date, $N_{\rm eq}=100$. As $M$ grows, it tends to 200, not infinity: extra names average away the idiosyncratic noise, but not the common date shock. This limit belongs to this stipulated mean-estimation problem, not to every panel model or neural network.

For a different problem, the mean of a stationary scalar AR(1)-like series with lag correlation $\rho^k$ has the large-$N$ approximation $N_{\rm eq}\approx N(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)$. At $N=5000$, $\rho=0.97$, it is about 76.142. The correlation must describe the relevant statistic’s series; a persistent feature does not automatically impose the same effective count on prediction errors. The approximation also requires an appropriate stationary covariance structure and a long enough sample.

Overlapping targets, common shocks and serial dependence can interact. Do **not** divide an AR(1) count by a second overlap factor without deriving the joint covariance. Negative correlation can even improve precision of a mean. A distribution change raises another question: old data may target a different population, which cannot generally be repaired by one universal row-count discount.

### When to use an information calculation

Use a dependence model or a justified resampling/error method for the statistic you report. Split by relevant dates and entities when the intended deployment requires it. More names, windows, augmented samples or random seeds do not automatically supply independent market evidence; nor does a parameter-to-rows ratio prove a model must fail.

## 2. Draw the information boundary in actual dates

A calendar drawn from memory is where off-by-one errors go to retire. Define a **decision anchor** $t$ as a feature record available at that day’s close. Its label is the return over $(t,t+h]$, fully available at close $t+h$. Freeze the fit at **close 12**, using candidate training anchors 1–12. The first held-out decision is at anchor 13; the frozen model scores anchors 13–16.

With $h=2$, a label is available for fitting only if $t+2\leq12$. Keep anchors 1–10. Exclude 11 and 12: their labels end at 13 and 14. There are **10 usable training anchors**, not a generic “horizon minus one” subtraction applied without a timing convention.

This prospective training set has no candidate rows after the test block. A post-test **embargo**, a buffer excluding otherwise eligible future-side training rows in some cross-validation designs, cannot remove rows that were never present. In a design that trains on both sides of an evaluation block, specify label intervals, feature availability, entity dependence and buffer direction explicitly. Purging removes forbidden overlap; an embargo is not proof that every remaining dependence or distribution shift vanished.

[Train-only preprocessing](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html) is not unique to deep learning. Scalers can leak in ridge regression too. Shuffling **already valid training windows** is not itself future leakage; the problem is mixing forbidden dates, labels, fitted statistics or hidden state across the boundary. Some stateful sequence tasks additionally require ordered batches and explicit resets.

[BatchNorm’s documented behaviour](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/generated/torch.nn.BatchNorm1d.html) also depends on its configuration: disabling running statistics can make evaluation use the current batch. LayerNorm does not remove the need to check which time positions enter each normalisation or attention operation.

### When to choose this evaluation design

Use an as-of prospective experiment to answer the deployment question posed here. Some stationary autoregressive problems permit other cross-validation designs under stated conditions; “all time-series K-fold leaks” is too broad. For rolling refits, declare the schedule in advance and refit only from information available at each new freeze date. Data from a previous test period may later become permitted historical training data under that schedule; it must not be used to retroactively tune the reported earlier decisions.

## 3. Regularisation is a trade-off to test

A smaller dial setting can reduce noise and also erase signal. Consider prediction at one fixed input. The true conditional mean is $f=2$ outcome units. Future observation noise is independent of training and has variance 1. Our fitted estimator takes values 0 or 4 with equal probability across hypothetical independent training samples.

Multiply that estimator by a shrink factor $a$. Its mean is $2a$ and its variance is $4a^2$. Under this specified squared-error setup,

$$
\mathbb E[(Y-\widehat f_a)^2]=\underbrace{4(a-1)^2}_{\text{squared bias}}+\underbrace{4a^2}_{\text{estimator variance}}+\underbrace{1}_{\text{new noise}}.
$$

Partial shrinkage helps **this** population; shrinking all the way to zero loses the gain. At $a=1.5$, error is $1+9+1=11$. We supplied the true distribution to verify the arithmetic. In practice that distribution is unknown, so validation must select the amount without turning the final test into another tuning set.

Dropout, small architectures, weight penalties and early stopping are different tools; none universally yields a small bias increase and a large variance reduction. Early stopping consumes validation information. [AdamW](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/generated/torch.optim.AdamW.html) applies decoupled weight decay; it is not generally identical to adding an L2 penalty to an adaptive optimiser’s loss. Augmentation must preserve a defensible financial label mechanism: a plausible-looking synthetic path is not another independent observed market history.

### An ensemble may share most of its error

If $K$ estimator errors have equal variance $\sigma_e^2$ and common pairwise correlation $\rho$ under a valid covariance structure, the equal-weight mean has variance

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar e)=\sigma_e^2\left[\rho+\frac{1-\rho}{K}\right].
$$

For $K=5$, $\rho=0.8$, this is $0.84\sigma_e^2$, not the $0.2\sigma_e^2$ obtained with independent errors. At $\rho=1$, averaging adds no variance benefit. The equation says nothing by itself about bias, net trading returns or new data availability.

### When to regularise or ensemble

Compare predeclared model variants on permitted validation data and measure the objective you care about. Averaging seeds is a specified model; picking the best seed is an additional selection step. A low training error is not proof of leakage, and a small training/test gap is not proof of useful prediction: two poor models can agree very closely.

## 4. Count selection, then name the statistic correctly

<MCQ client:visible pretest
 question={"Twenty independent calibrated null tests each pass with probability 0.025. Which probability is being computed by 1−.975^20?"}
 options={[{"text": "The chance at least one passes under that joint null model", "correct": true}, {"text": "The chance the best architecture has causal skill"}, {"text": "The DSR obtained by subtracting expected maximum Sharpe"}, {"text": "The chance of positive future returns in a changing market"}]}
 explanation={"The family event is the complement of all twenty failures. Independence is stipulated, not established by a run log."}
/>

Suppose each of $N$ **independent null tests** has probability 0.025 of passing a chosen one-sided threshold. These are invented, correctly calibrated null tests, not a claim about actual architecture runs. The probability that at least one passes is

$$
P(\text{any pass})=1-(1-0.025)^N.
$$

At $N=20$, it is $1-0.975^{20}=39.731232\%$; at 200, about 99.367700%. These are family-level false-positive probabilities for this independent model. Identical copied tests would not multiply the chance this way. Correlated architecture runs require a dependence-aware analysis; logging 200 runs does not establish 200 independent tests.

<div id="search-null-example" className="scroll-mt-24" />

<TemporalModelAudit client:visible mode="search" title="Selection probability under a stipulated independent null" caption="Invented calibrated independent null candidates. Log choices of features, architectures, windows, costs, stopping epochs, seeds and trading rules when their results influence selection. A nested validation process separates fitting and choosing from the final reported evaluation. Repeatedly consulting the final set to pick changes makes it selection data; an untouched later evaluation can test the resulting procedure. Purging does not undo model selection.

### PSR, a search benchmark and DSR

The [Deflated Sharpe Ratio paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf) adjusts a probabilistic-Sharpe calculation for a specified search benchmark. It does **not** define DSR as observed Sharpe minus $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln N}$. That square-root expression is only a leading asymptotic description of an expected maximum under a particular independent Gaussian model; an expected maximum is neither a significance cutoff nor a probability.

In a suitable normal approximation, write the probabilistic Sharpe statistic against benchmark $S_0$ as

$$
z=\frac{(\widehat S-S_0)\sqrt{T-1}}{\sqrt{1-\widehat\gamma_3\widehat S+(\widehat\gamma_4-1)\widehat S^2/4}},\qquad \mathrm{PSR}(S_0)\approx\Phi(z).
$$

Here $T$ counts **return observations**, $\widehat\gamma_3$ is skewness, $\widehat\gamma_4$ is ordinary kurtosis (3 for a normal distribution), and $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. Sharpe and the benchmark use the same return frequency. If $S_0$ is a search-adjusted expected-maximum benchmark under the defended trial model, the corresponding statistic is called DSR. Serial dependence, nonstationarity, moment estimation, adaptive selection and trial dependence need separate treatment; the formula does not fix them automatically.

For a separate invented monthly net-return record, take $\widehat S=0.10$, $T=101$, skewness 0 and kurtosis 3. Against a **stipulated** threshold $S_0=0.05$, $z=0.5/\sqrt{1.005}\approx0.498755$ and PSR is about 0.691024. This threshold alone has no search provenance, so this calculation is not yet DSR.

Now supply a search model: there are exactly **two independent null monthly-Sharpe estimates**, each normally distributed with mean zero and standard deviation $\sigma_{\rm trial}=0.10$. For this two-candidate normal model, the expected maximum is exactly $\sigma_{\rm trial}/\sqrt\pi=0.05641895835\ldots$. One way to see the identity is $\max(A,B)=(A+B+|A-B|)/2$; the centred normal difference has variance $2\sigma_{\rm trial}^2$ and mean absolute value $2\sigma_{\rm trial}/\sqrt\pi$.

Using that derived benchmark gives $z\approx0.434725$ and DSR about 0.668119 under the stated approximation. The monthly units match in $\widehat S$, $\sigma_{\rm trial}$ and $S_0$. The number of candidates is two; the number of returns is 101. Do not swap them.

DSR versus an expected maximum is **not familywise error control**, and neither score is a causal probability of skill or the probability of earning future profit. The independent normal trial model is supplied for this calculation, not identified by the fact that two networks were trained on the same market data.

### When to use a selection adjustment

Use it when results influence which model or policy is reported, with a search log and a defensible dependence/uncertainty model. Prefer a predeclared budget and fresh evaluation over a post-hoc subtraction presented as a certificate. Do not manufacture a numerical “effective trial count” without explaining how it was estimated.

Mean squared error is 0.01 pp², versus $[1^2+(-1)^2+1^2+(-1)^2]/4=1$ pp² for a zero forecast. That is a large predictive-loss improvement **on these four invented observations**; it says nothing about how often this record would recur.

A fixed policy takes the forecast’s sign, opens USD 10,000 of exposure each period and **fully closes it each period**. It starts with cash USD 20,000, sufficient for the stipulated trades; shorts are feasible, cash earns zero and there is no funding cost, tax, spread or impact beyond the explicit invented fee. Each period’s gross P&L is $10{,}000\times1\%=100$ USD, so gross total is 400.

Charge **100 basis points per leg on the fixed entry notional**, including that same declared base on the exit leg. This is an intentionally costly invented fee schedule, not a claim about a venue. Each leg costs $10{,}000\times0.01=100$ USD; four round trips have eight legs and cost 800. Net P&L is $400-800=-400$ USD; ending cash is 19,600. The no-trade cash policy earns zero with no fees. A better MSE therefore lost the economic comparison.

### When to compare model families

For a prediction claim, compare the same target and as-of data using the same metric. For a trading claim, specify position rules, universe, cash/funding, costs, capacity, constraints and terminal treatment. Include no-trade and relevant simple or linear baselines, then justified tree and deep alternatives with disclosed tuning resources. Report uncertainty of **paired differences** on shared evaluation periods rather than treating every row as a new independent win.

The [2022 tabular benchmark](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/0378c7692da36807bdec87ab043cdadc-Abstract.html) found strong tree performance on its selected tasks; the [2023 study](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/f06d5ebd4ff40b40dd97e30cee632123-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf) found that tuning and dataset properties often matter as much as the model-family distinction. Those are benchmark-specific results, not a universal finance ranking. Sequence or text structure motivates testing an architecture, not declaring it the winner. Sparse text features with a simple fitted model are also legitimate baselines when they answer the same task.

## 6. Monitor a frozen procedure without inventing a diagnosis

<MCQ client:visible pretest

A live drop can flag a problem without identifying its cause. As a separate illustration, suppose a historical period estimates mean information coefficient (IC, a specified cross-sectional prediction/return correlation) at 0.040 and a later, disjoint period at 0.018. Assume each mean’s standard error is 0.015, the two estimates are independent and a normal approximation is suitable. These uncertainties are supplied for the example, not inferred from the point estimates.

The difference is $0.018-0.040=-0.022$, with assumed standard error $\sqrt{0.015^2+0.015^2}=0.0212132$. Its approximate 95% interval is

$$
-0.022\pm1.96(0.0212132)=[-0.0635779,\ 0.0195779].
$$

That interval includes zero under these assumptions. It does not establish equality, rule out economic harm or diagnose leakage, overfitting or a changed market mechanism. If estimates share dates, names or fitted models, the covariance belongs in the difference’s uncertainty. Real IC series may require dependence-aware methods; repeatedly checking many dates adds a sequential-selection problem.

A prospective evaluation specifies a training and refit schedule, inner validation choices and a final evaluation window before inspecting its outcomes. A held-out loss can be due to chance, costs, implementation or distribution change even after a careful audit. Conversely, a passing score does not prove a stable mechanism.

### When to investigate or change a model

Use predeclared operational and risk limits, data-quality checks and a documented response. Paper trading can expose timing and pipeline failures, but simulated orders do not demonstrate actual queue fills, market impact or funding availability. A chosen IC level or decline is not a universal kill threshold. If outcomes lead you to revise the model or limits, evaluate that revised procedure on new information; do not relabel the data used to choose it as untouched validation.
