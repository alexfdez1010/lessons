## Additional contract and outcome cases

These are invented cases with no fees or taxes. All amounts and legal priority assumptions are stipulated for the exercise.

**Priority:** one issuer has 600 available after all other costs and claims. It owes 500 on a senior debt claim and 500 on a subordinated claim. Apply the stated strict ordering with no other rights. Candidates give subordinated recovery / recovery percentage.

**Currency:** spend 1,000 euros to buy at par a one-year bond with 1,000-dollar face value, when one dollar costs one euro. Receive 50 dollars of interest and 1,000 dollars of principal together at year end. Each dollar then converts to 0.90 euro. Euro price inflation over that year is 5%; there is no separate currency protection. Candidates give final nominal euros / final cash in start-date euro purchasing power.

## Key Takeaways

<Callout variant="success" title="What to carry out of this topic">
- **A bond is debt.** A plain fixed-rate contract schedules coupons based on face value and principal at maturity. Market price does not reset the fixed coupon promise, but default can change the amounts actually received. Face value need not equal the price paid; seniority, early sale, currency conversion and inflation also affect the outcome.
- **Price and yield use a defined schedule.** For the same unchanged cash flows, a higher required yield lowers price. Current yield is annual scheduled coupon divided by price; YTM fits the entire scheduled stream. Neither guarantees the realised result. Coupon reinvestment affects compound terminal wealth, while default or an early sale can change the received cash flows.
- **The yield curve needs a definition.** State the currency, selected universe, observation time and rate conventions. Coupon YTM, spot and par curves answer different questions. Name the spread pair and measure its difference in percentage points. Expectations and possibly negative term premia both affect the slope; historical recession signals depend on the model and horizon, and do not decide a retail loan contract.
- **Duration is rate sensitivity:** $\%\,\Delta\text{Price} \approx -D \times \Delta y$, a percent move per 1% yield change (not dollars). **Lower coupons and longer maturities mean longer duration** — more interest-rate risk.
- **Convexity is the curve duration's straight line misses:** $\%\,\Delta\text{Price} \approx -D \times \Delta y + \tfrac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2$. The square makes it **always positive — gains bigger, losses smaller** — and it matters most for **large** yield moves. Duration alone is *not* exact for big swings.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="info" title="Between-coupon settlement">
A quote per100 face must first be converted to cash. Dirty equals clean plus the signed coupon-accrual adjustment in matching units, before separate costs. Use settlement, the stated day count and the payment rights: ex-coupon can mean negative accrual, while zero-coupon means no periodic-coupon allocation here. A cash coupon receipt is not the entire investment gain.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="info" title="Indexed payments and inflation compensation">
Read the reference index, payment-date ratio, lag and issue-specific floor. An original-principal maturity floor does not reimburse every purchase price or floor every coupon. Real-rate price risk remains. The exact factor-ratio breakeven belongs to the stated matched zero-coupon comparison; a market nominal-minus-TIPS spread can also contain risk and relative-liquidity components.
</Callout>
