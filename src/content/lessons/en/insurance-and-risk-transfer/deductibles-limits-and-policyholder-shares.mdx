Two policies both mention a 500 deductible, 20% participation and a 2,000 limit. They can still pay different amounts. The missing information is what is subtracted, what is multiplied and which amount is capped.

This lesson uses independent invented contracts in EUR. “Policyholder percentage participation” means the part of a covered loss retained by the buyer. It is not a division of a policy between several insurers. The US word *coinsurance* also has different health and property-policy meanings; importing the word without its formula is an excellent way to misread a bill.

## Apply the deductible before the percentage share

<MCQ client:visible pretest
question={"Does a 2,000 insurer-payment cap limit all buyer loss to 2,000?"}
options={[{"text": "No; retained loss can keep growing after the cap binds", "correct": true}, {"text": "Yes; buyer and insurer must always have identical caps"}, {"text": "Only if each loss is larger than the annual premium"}]}
explanation={"The cap names the insurer payment, not buyer out-of-pocket loss."}
/>

Let $L$ be an eligible covered loss. Contract A says: subtract a **per-claim deductible** $d=500$; apply a **20% policyholder share** to the positive remainder; then cap the **insurer's payment** at $M=2000$. Its rule is

$$I_A(L)=\min\left(0.8\max(L-500,0),2000\right),\qquad R_A(L)=L-I_A(L).$$

For $L=4000$, the post-deductible amount is 3,500. The buyer's percentage share of that amount is $0.2(3500)=700$. Before the cap the insurer would pay $0.8(3500)=2800$; the payment cap reduces that to 2,000. The buyer retains $4000-2000=2000$: deductible 500, percentage share 700 and another 800 above the cap. A separate premium would be additional.

For a 1,000 loss, payment is $0.8(1000-500)=400$ and retention 600; the cap does not bind. At a 300 loss the deductible absorbs everything, so payment is zero and retention 300, not 500.

### Use the full piecewise rule

At small losses the deductible dominates; in the middle the percentage share matters; at large losses the payment cap dominates. An insurer-payment limit of 2,000 is not a cap on the buyer's total out-of-pocket loss. Losses outside the definition of eligible $L$ would require a separate coverage analysis.

## Ask which amount the limit caps

<MCQ client:visible pretest
question={"If a cap moves inside the percentage multiplication, must payout stay equal?"}
options={[{"text": "No; the contract is capping a different amount", "correct": true}, {"text": "Yes; multiplication and a binding minimum always commute"}, {"text": "Yes; the word limit fixes one universal calculation"}]}
explanation={"Compare min(.8 x,M) with .8 min(x,M)."}
/>

Order is part of the contract, like brackets in arithmetic. Contract B instead caps the post-deductible amount at 2,000 **before** applying the 20% share:

$$I_B(L)=0.8\min\left(\max(L-500,0),2000\right).$$

At the same 4,000 loss, the inner minimum is $\min(3500,2000)=2000$. Insurer payment is $0.8(2000)=1600$ and retained loss is $4000-1600=2400$. Contract A paid 2,000, a 400 difference. The numerical “limit” happens to be the same; what it limits is different.

The difference disappears when the pre-cap amount is small enough: at loss 1,000 both pay 400. Equality in one worked example does not prove two contract rules equivalent for every loss.

### Compare the wording and the same loss states

Reconcile a quote, summary and full policy before using a calculator. Do not silently choose the more generous order. Deductibles may be fixed amounts, percentages or subject to other clauses; limits may apply per item, event, period or covered category. The [NAIC glossary](https://content.naic.org/glossary-insurance-terms) illustrates why terms need their product context. Our two formulas are teaching contracts, not a claim about standard market order.

## Track an aggregate limit across claims

<MCQ client:visible pretest
question={"Two losses occur within one annual payment limit. What must carry forward?"}
options={[{"text": "The limit remaining after earlier insurer payments", "correct": true}, {"text": "The full original limit separately for every event"}, {"text": "Only premium already paid; claim payments do not count"}]}
explanation={"Aggregate capacity is consumed over its stated period."}
/>

A **per-claim rule** resets for each claim; an **aggregate limit** caps total insurer payments over a stated period. Put a counter beside the contract rather than applying the full annual limit to every event.

Contract C covers two eligible losses of 1,200 each in the same policy year. Each has its own 500 deductible, no percentage share, and no separate binding per-claim cap. Total insurer payments during that year cannot exceed 1,000.

The first loss produces $1200-500=700$, leaving $1000-700=300$ of the annual limit. The second would also produce 700 before the aggregate constraint, but pays only $\min(700,300)=300$. Payments total 1,000; total retained loss is $2400-1000=1400$. The buyer retains 500 on the first claim and 900 on the second. Premium is excluded from this loss-only calculation.

<InsuranceRiskLab client:visible mode="aggregate" title="Consume the annual limit across two claims" />

If the second loss were 400, its own deductible would leave no payment and the unused 300 limit would remain unused. An unused limit is not a refund. A new period's reset, reinstatement of a limit, multiple losses treated as one event, and annual versus per-event deductibles all require explicit wording.

### Follow the remaining limit as a state variable

Use an ordered claim ledger when there can be several events or several covered items. Confirm whose payments consume the limit and when it resets. Never infer that two policies with equal annual premiums protect the same sequence of losses.
