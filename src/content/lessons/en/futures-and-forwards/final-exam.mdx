This final exam covers eight lessons: contract obligations and payoffs, clearing, margin, cost-of-carry pricing, curve shapes, rolling and the people using these markets. It also tests local basis, hedge direction, comparable quote units, whole contracts and a minimum-variance benchmark. Use only the assumptions stated in each question; a useful hedge does not remove every source of risk.

For carry questions, keep compounding, cash-payment dates, executable quote sides and financing/short-sale access explicit. Convenience is not a cash receipt. A current curve shape alone does not guarantee a later strategy return.

Settlement questions distinguish the last variation increment from cumulative P&L and from a full physical invoice. A margin return already contains retained variation; value posted, added and returned cash at common dates when financing matters. Use the specified deliverable or cash reference and its observation/payment calendar. A qualified terminal convergence relation neither forces a monotone path nor removes every local basis. Trading and delivery-allocation deadlines need not coincide.
