Buying oil for use next month involves storage, financing and delivery. Buying a futures position involves contract dates, collateral and possible renewal into another contract. Those differences mean that a commodity’s spot price and an investor’s return can move differently.

Building on futures and forwards, this course connects physical-market constraints to the futures curve, roll returns and portfolio exposure. It also examines when real assets may help with inflation risk and when they may fail to do so.

- **Commodity futures in practice** — contract specs, hedgers versus speculators, physical
  delivery, the basis, and why most longs never intend to take a single barrel.
- **Storage & convenience yield** — the full cost-of-carry formula, why storable and
  non-storable commodities behave differently, and how convenience yield flips a curve.
- **Contango, backwardation & roll yield** — the shape of the futures curve, and the
  roll that quietly makes or loses money for a passive long every month.
- **Seasonality & commodity indices** — calendar patterns in gas, fuel and grain, and how
  index products (GSCI, BCOM) bundle spot, roll, and collateral into one total return.
- **Gold & the oil market** — gold as a monetary, non-yielding store of value, and the
  real plumbing of crude: benchmarks, OPEC, the supply curve, and refining spreads.
- **Real assets & inflation** — how commodities, gold, real estate and inflation-linked
  bonds differ as inflation exposures, and why their protection depends on the horizon and shock.
