The same household budget can be affected by a higher food bill, a change in borrowing rates and a fall in income. **Economics** studies how people and institutions make choices when resources are limited, and how those choices interact.

Starting with money and everyday trade-offs, the course develops supply and demand, inflation and growth before examining central banks, government budgets and international trade. New terms and chart conventions are introduced as they are needed.

- **What economics even is** — scarcity, trade-offs, and the "opportunity cost" hiding behind every choice.
- **Supply and demand** — how buyers and sellers interact to determine market prices, and what makes prices jump or crash.
- **Inflation** — how a broad rise in prices differs from one product becoming more expensive, how it's measured, and the difference between *real* and *nominal*.
- **GDP and the business cycle** — how we measure a whole economy, and why it booms and busts in waves.
- **Central banks and interest rates** — who sets the price of money, and how a rate hike ripples into your life.
- **Fiscal policy and trade** — what governments do with taxes, spending, deficits, and dealing across borders.
