A strategy earns small gains for years and then loses more in one event than it earned throughout that record. Looking only at the average obscures the exposure that produced it. Nassim Taleb's *Incerto* asks how to make decisions when rare events and model error can dominate the result.

Building on fat tails, risk of ruin and behavioural biases, this course examines Taleb's arguments and the conditions under which they help:

- **Fooled by randomness** — separate a favourable outcome from evidence of skill, including survivorship and selection effects.
- **Black swans and Extremistan** — examine how rare observations can dominate an aggregate and what a limited sample can miss.
- **Fragile, robust and antifragile** — distinguish being harmed by variability, resisting it and benefiting from it over a specified range.
- **Convexity and the barbell** — compare payoff shapes and combinations of protected capital with limited risky exposure, including their costs.
- **Skin in the game** — ask who makes a decision, who gains and who bears its losses.
- **Ergodicity and ruin** — specify the observable, distinguish stationarity from ergodicity, apply the log-increment law of large numbers, and compare sizing and failure models.
- **Lindy and via negativa** — assess survival-based reasoning and the removal of fragile dependencies without treating longevity as a guarantee.
