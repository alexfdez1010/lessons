A foreign asset rises in its local currency, but that currency falls against yours. Your return at home combines both changes. In the **foreign-exchange (FX) market**, every quote compares one currency with another, so identifying the base and quote currency comes before calculating a gain.

The course starts with pairs, spreads and cross rates, then connects forwards to interest-rate differentials. It examines carry trades, central-bank intervention, exchange-rate regimes and the costs and limits of hedging a global portfolio.

- **How a quote works** — base vs quote currency, pips, bid/ask spreads, and how to
  build a cross rate when no direct quote exists.
- **Spot vs forward FX** — usual T+2 settlement and currency-pair exceptions, forward points, FX swaps, and
  non-deliverable forwards for currencies you can't freely move.
- **Covered interest parity** — match dated currency cash flows, derive the ideal forward, and test executable borrowing/deposit and bid–ask prices with their funding constraints.
- **Uncovered parity and carry** — separate a covered forward from a physical expected-return hypothesis; calculate exact quote conversions, nonlinear expectations, financed P&L and intermediate funding risk.
- **Central banks, pegs & crises** — intervention, fixed vs floating regimes, the
  *impossible trinity*, and the anatomy of a currency crisis from 1992 to 1997.
- **Currency in a portfolio** — how unhedged foreign holdings smuggle in FX risk,
  what hedging actually costs, and when to bother.

- **Purchasing power and real exchange rates** — comparable basket-price benchmarks, exact relative-inflation factors, quote direction, CPI rebasing and the limits of interpreting a benchmark gap as a currency forecast.
