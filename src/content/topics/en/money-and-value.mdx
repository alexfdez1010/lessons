You earn \$1,200, spend \$900 and see \$300 left in your account. Those three numbers introduce income, expenses and a balance. To understand what the balance means, you also need to know how payments move and how they are recorded.

This is the starting point of the finance path. The lessons define the vocabulary as it appears and use purchases, wages and household accounts to explain:

- **Barter & why money exists** — the maddening problem of needing someone who wants
  your bread *and* sells the shoes you want, and how money fixes it.
- **The bedrock vocabulary** — money, payment, interest, account, balance and the
  rest, each defined the first time you meet it.
- **How a payment really happens** — who pays whom, what flows back, and why
  "Approved" on a card reader doesn't yet mean the money has moved.
- **Income vs. expense** — following money through your own life: in, out, and what
  the leftover (or the shortfall) means.
- **Saving vs. borrowing** — what to do with a surplus, and what a shortfall costs.
- **The ledger** — finance's most underrated invention: the humble running list of
  who has what. Your bank balance is just a line on one.

- **Bank statements and reconciliation** — rebuild posted balances, distinguish holds and explain differences with evidence.

- **Cash-flow budgets and liquidity buffers** — put receipts and payments on dates, distinguish wealth from usable cash and test the reserves required by explicit scenarios.
