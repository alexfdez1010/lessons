A swap is worth a positive amount to you today. If the counterparty defaults, replacing that trade may cost money, even though no payment was due this morning. Tomorrow, market moves could reverse which party is exposed. **Counterparty credit risk** depends on both the chance of default and the exposure at that time.

Using the bond, credit and swap prerequisites, this course measures exposure through time, reduces it with netting and collateral, and studies the valuation adjustments and clearing arrangements applied to the remaining risks.

- **What counterparty risk is** — why a derivative is a *two-way* credit exposure that a loan is not, the difference between **settlement** and **replacement** risk, and how 2008 turned a footnote into a front-office discipline.
- **Measuring exposure** — current exposure, **expected exposure (EE)** and its average **EPE**, and **potential future exposure (PFE)**; the *diffusion-vs-amortisation* tug-of-war that gives a swap its hump-shaped profile and an FX forward its rising one.
- **Netting & collateral** — how **close-out netting** collapses a whole book to a single number, and how a **CSA** with **variation margin**, **initial margin**, **thresholds**, **minimum transfer amounts** and **haircuts** crushes the exposure that is left.
- **CVA — the price of default** — the **credit valuation adjustment** as the market price of counterparty risk, its three ingredients (exposure × default probability × loss given default), and the mirror-image **DVA** on your own credit.
- **The XVA family** — the post-2008 alphabet soup: **FVA** (funding), **MVA** (initial-margin funding), **KVA** (capital) and **ColVA**, assembled into the waterfall that turns a risk-free price into the price a desk actually charges.
- **Wrong-way risk & clearing** — when exposure and default probability rise *together* (and the right-way case where they offset), and the great post-crisis migration to **central counterparties**, their **default waterfalls**, and the **SA-CCR** capital rules and **uncleared-margin** plumbing that govern the rest.
