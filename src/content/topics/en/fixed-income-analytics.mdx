For a plain fixed-rate bond, the promised payments are known, but their present value changes with the discount curve. Pricing those payments and estimating their sensitivity are separate tasks: the first gives a value under chosen assumptions, while duration, convexity and DV01 describe particular rate changes.

This expert continuation of Bonds & Rates develops those calculations, constructs spot and forward curves, and applies them to credit analysis, liability matching and hedging. A hedge’s effectiveness depends on which risks the model and instruments cover.

- **Bond pricing math** — turn a stream of coupons plus a face value into a present value, define yield to maturity precisely, and see why price and yield are locked in an inverse, convex dance.
- **Duration** — Macaulay duration as the cash-flow balance point, modified duration as the price's percentage sensitivity to yield, and **DV01** as the dollar bleed per basis point.
- **Convexity** — the second-order correction that fixes duration's straight-line lie, and how positive convexity changes a plain bond’s response to yield moves.
- **Bootstrapping the yield curve** — strip coupon bonds into pure **spot (zero) rates**, then extract the **forward rates** the market has priced for the future, all by no-arbitrage.
- **Term-structure models** — a first quantitative look at **Vasicek** and **CIR**: short rates that *mean-revert* on a leash, the bridge into stochastic processes.
- **Credit spreads and default risk** — distinguish promised yields, physical expected losses and pricing measures; specify recovery, convert intensity to cumulative PD, and separate dependence-driven joint tails from fixed marginal means.
- **Immunization & hedging** — match duration (and convexity) to a liability so a rate move barely scratches your surplus, and compute the **DV01 hedge ratio** that offsets a chosen first-order rate exposure.

- **Key rate duration and curve hedges** — specify node coordinates and interpolation, separate level, slope and curvature, and solve several bucket exposures while checking rounding, conditioning and residual risks.

- **Cash-flow matching and bond ladders** — size coupon and principal receipts backwards, fund dated cash balances and costs, distinguish duration matching from renewal, and stress promises and liabilities.
