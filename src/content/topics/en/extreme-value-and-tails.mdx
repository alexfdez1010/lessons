A 99% value-at-risk estimate identifies a loss quantile. It does not tell you the average loss beyond that point. **Tail modelling** studies those extremes and the dependence that can make several positions suffer together. VaR and Monte Carlo do not require a Gaussian distribution; the distribution and dependence assumptions chosen for them matter.

This course develops heavy-tail models and extreme value theory, then connects them to expected shortfall and stress scenarios. Sparse extreme observations and model uncertainty remain part of every estimate.

- **Fat tails vs. the Gaussian** — why returns aren't normal, what **kurtosis** measures, and how badly the bell curve underestimates the extremes it can't see.
- **Power laws & the tail index** — the one number $\alpha$ that governs tail weight, the eerie **scale invariance** of a power law, and the **Hill estimator** that reads $\alpha$ off real data.
- **Extreme Value Theory** — both great results: **block maxima** → the **Generalized Extreme Value** distribution, and **peaks-over-threshold** → the **Generalized Pareto Distribution**, unified by one **shape parameter** $\xi$ that tells you if your tail is bounded, exponential, or heavy.
- **Expected Shortfall beyond VaR** — the average loss *given* you're in the tail, why ES is **coherent** (it respects diversification via **subadditivity**, which VaR can violate), and how an **EVT-based ES** extrapolates past the data.
- **Copulas & tail dependence** — why correlation is a fair-weather friend, and how a **t-copula** captures positive asymptotic tail dependence that a non-degenerate Gaussian copula lacks.
- **Stress testing** — scenario and historical tests, **reverse stress testing** (working backwards from ruin to the shock that causes it), and the honest limits of all of it.
