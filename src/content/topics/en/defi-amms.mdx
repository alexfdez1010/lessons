In a constant-product **automated market maker (AMM)**, traders exchange tokens with a pool funded by liquidity providers. A formula relates the two reserves: adding one token and withdrawing the other moves the pool along its pricing curve. An order book is not required, but capital and counterparties still exist.

Starting from Ethereum and stablecoins, the course follows that reserve change to the trader’s execution price and the liquidity provider’s gains or losses.

- **Why AMMs exist** — how trading-against-a-pool differs from trading-against-a-person, and the trade-offs that make pools useful on-chain.
- **The constant-product formula x·y=k** — the invariant in the basic constant-product model, and why a trade just walks the pool along a curve.
- **Liquidity providers & LP tokens** — what it means to seed a pool, what your LP token represents, and how fees accrue to your share.
- **Slippage & price impact** — why a big trade gets a worse price, and what "slippage tolerance" actually protects you from.
- **Impermanent loss** — derive a fee-free holding comparison, distinguish currency and denominators, account for fees once and understand where concentrated-range inventory needs a different model.

- **Concentrated liquidity and range risk** — calculate actual token quantities from bounds and L, retain unused wallet assets, compare equal-token range budgets, allocate fees along an active path and fund a range change without resetting the holding benchmark.
