A long straddle can lose money even when the underlying moves: the movement may be too small to cover the premium. A delta-hedged option adds the path of moves, hedge timing and transaction costs to that comparison. Having a view on volatility requires specifying the instrument and how its profit is measured.

Building on option pricing, the Greeks and time series, the course compares implied and realized volatility, variance exposure and the costs of protection:

- **Realized vs implied volatility** — the two volatilities that matter, how each is measured and annualized, and why the *gap* between them is the whole game.
- **Variance & vol swaps** — the cleanest instruments for trading vol directly, why a variance swap is **long convexity**, and how it links to a strip of options.
- **The VIX & its term structure** — what the "fear index" actually measures, why its futures curve sits in **contango** most of the time, and how rolling VIX futures can create a drag in futures-based products.
- **The volatility risk premium** — why implied vol sits *above* realized on average, how sellers harvest that premium, and why it's *picking up pennies in front of a steamroller*.
- **Straddles & strangles** — the workhorse long-vol structures, their break-evens, and the daily tug-of-war between **vega** and **theta**.
- **Dispersion trading** — sell index vol, buy single-name vol, and you've built a bet on **correlation** — an exposure to correlation whose sign depends on which variance legs are bought and sold.
- **Tail hedging in practice** — engineer a **convex** payoff from deep out-of-the-money puts that has a recurring premium cost and pays according to strikes, expiry, path and hedge size.
