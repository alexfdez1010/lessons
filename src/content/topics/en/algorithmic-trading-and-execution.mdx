You need to buy a large position. Trading immediately crosses the spread and can push the price up; spreading the order out leaves you exposed while you wait. **Execution** is the decision about how to divide that order, where to send it and how quickly to complete it.

This course measures those costs before comparing execution algorithms and optimal-control models. The aim is to connect a strategy’s paper return to the return its trades could deliver after realistic costs.

- **The cost of trading** — explicit costs (commissions and fees) versus *implicit* ones (spread, impact, timing and opportunity), and the **trader's dilemma**: trade fast and pay impact, trade slow and bleed to drift.
- **Implementation shortfall** — the difference between the decision-price benchmark and the realised result: paper return minus real return, decomposed into delay, spread, impact, timing, and opportunity cost.
- **Market impact & the square-root law** — why pushing twice the size does *not* cost twice as much, the difference between **temporary** and **permanent** impact, and the empirical $\sigma\sqrt{Q/V}$ approximation and its relevance to capacity.
- **Execution algorithms** — the workhorses every desk runs: **TWAP** (by the clock), **VWAP** (by the volume curve), **POV** (by participation rate), and **IS / arrival-price** algos that race alpha against impact.
- **Optimal execution** — the **Almgren–Chriss** frontier: trading impact against timing risk, how risk aversion and a decaying signal set the optimal speed, and why there is no free trajectory.
- **Transaction-cost analysis (TCA)** — measuring execution quality honestly: arrival, VWAP, and close benchmarks, slippage attribution, and closing the feedback loop on your own algos.
- **Backtesting & HFT** — the execution traps that make a backtest lie (transaction-cost-naive fills, capacity, alpha decay) and a tour of the **high-frequency** strategies — market making, latency and statistical arbitrage — that live on the other side of your order.
