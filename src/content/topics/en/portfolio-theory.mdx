Two assets each have 20% annual volatility. Split the money equally and assume their returns are uncorrelated: the portfolio's volatility is about 14.1%, rather than 20%. If they move together perfectly, that reduction disappears. Diversification depends on how holdings interact, not just how many you have.

The course builds this comparison into portfolio theory, keeping estimated returns, correlations and model assumptions visible:

- **Why diversification works** — the split between company-specific risk you can reduce and market risk you're stuck with.
- **Correlation and covariance** — the engine that makes it tick, and the exact portfolio-volatility formula showing correlation changes the benefit of a given mix.
- **Combining assets** — watch the risk–return curve *bend inward* as you mix holdings.
- **The efficient frontier** — and its minimum-variance tip, the best return for each level of risk.
- **The Capital Market Line** — add a risk-free asset to draw the capital allocation line through the model's tangency portfolio.
- **CAPM** — beta, the market risk premium, and the Security Market Line that prices systematic risk.

- **Rebalancing rules and weight drift** — calculate the trades or cash flows that restore a target, then account for rules, costs and changing risks.
