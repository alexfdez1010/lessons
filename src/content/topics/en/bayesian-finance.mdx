A strategy wins several trades. Is that strong evidence of skill, or a plausible run of luck? **Bayesian inference** combines an initial probability model, the **prior**, with the **likelihood** of the observed data to produce an updated model, the **posterior**. The result depends on both the evidence and the assumptions.

The course develops that update from natural-frequency examples to return estimates and portfolio views. It also shows how to express uncertainty and examine sensitivity to the prior.

- **Prior, likelihood, posterior** — the three objects every Bayesian argument is made of, and the rule that fuses what you believed before with what the data says.
- **The base-rate fallacy** — the error of confusing P(data given hypothesis) with P(hypothesis given data). Reason in **natural frequencies** ("of 1000 signals…") and watch a 90%-accurate test still spit out mostly false alarms.
- **Conjugate priors & sequential updating** — turn updating into one-line arithmetic: a **Beta** belief about a win rate sharpens by just adding wins and losses, where today's posterior is tomorrow's prior.
- **Precision-weighted estimates** — the **Normal–Normal** update, where a posterior mean is a precision-weighted blend of prior and data: noisy data barely nudges you, abundant data takes over.
- **Shrinkage & Black–Litterman** — the conditions under which James–Stein shrinkage improves estimation, and the model that blends market equilibrium returns with investor views to tame the "error-maximizing" optimizer.
- **Credible intervals vs. confidence intervals** — interpret posterior probability within the specified model, and distinguish that statement from frequentist coverage.
- **MCMC** — sample a posterior with no closed form by wandering: propose, compare, accept-or-stay, repeat, and check convergence before using the samples to approximate the posterior.
