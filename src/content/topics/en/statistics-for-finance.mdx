A strategy wins on 55 of 100 trades. That record is an observation; deciding whether the strategy has an edge requires the size of gains and losses, sampling uncertainty and how the trades were selected. Trying hundreds of strategies before reporting the best one changes the question again.

Assuming familiarity with returns and volatility but no formal statistics, this course builds the tools needed to reason about those uncertainties:

- **Probability and random variables** — events, conditional probability, independence, and how a PDF/CDF describes a quantity you can't predict.
- **Distributions in finance** — the normal, the lognormal used to model positive prices, and the fat tails that wreck models built on the bell curve.
- **Expectation, variance and moments** — mean, variance, and the skew and kurtosis that say how lopsided and how tail-heavy returns are.
- **Covariance, correlation and regression** — how two assets move together, and how to fit a line that estimates beta and explains R².
- **The Central Limit Theorem and estimation** — when normalized averages approach a normal distribution, what a standard error is, and how to put a confidence interval on an estimate.
- **Hypothesis testing** — null vs alternative, p-values, false positives, false negatives and the multiple-testing problem created by searching many strategies.
