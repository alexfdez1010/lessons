An execution agent must buy 10,000 shares before a deadline. Buying quickly reduces the chance of missing the order, but may move the price; waiting preserves flexibility while the market can move away. Each choice changes the next decision.

Reinforcement learning represents that sequence through states, actions and rewards. This course compares policies for execution, market making and sizing, while checking what the reward and simulator actually encourage:

- **Markets as a Markov decision process** — the formal language of RL (state, action, reward, transition, policy, return, discounting), how trading decisions can be modelled sequentially and when their impact changes future states.
- **Reward design and its pitfalls** — reconcile cash and inventory, charge costs once and settle the terminal position. Distinguish risk preferences, discounting and local Sharpe signals; derive when potential shaping preserves the objective and audit reward against economic outcomes.
- **Value-based vs policy-gradient methods** — the two great families: learning *what each situation is worth* (Q-learning, DQN) versus learning *the policy directly* (policy gradients, PPO, actor–critic). When each fits, why continuous actions push you toward policy methods, and the exploration-versus-exploitation problem under transaction costs.
- **Optimal execution as an RL problem** — a concrete application: slicing a large order to balance market impact against timing risk. How this relates to the closed-form Almgren–Chriss solution, what RL adds (and what it merely re-derives), and how to read a learned execution policy.
- **Market-making agents** — quoting both sides of the book while managing inventory: the inventory-versus-spread trade-off, the Avellaneda–Stoikov skeleton, adverse selection, and why a market-making reward is a minefield of perverse incentives.
- **The sim-to-real gap** — the lesson that humbles everything above: why a policy that performs well in simulation can fail in live trading. Non-stationarity, an imperfect simulator, the agent overfitting its own environment, and the deployment discipline that keeps you honest.
