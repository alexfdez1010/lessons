A banknote convertible into gold and a modern fiat banknote can both pay a bill, but the promises and institutions behind them differ. This course follows how those arrangements changed under war, inflation, international imbalances and technological development.

Starting from what money is, it concentrates on the twentieth century and the origins of Bitcoin. The timeline connects each monetary change to the constraint it addressed and the trade-offs it introduced.

- **The gold standard** — when a pound or a dollar was a fixed weight of metal, and how that golden chain both disciplined nations and dragged them into depression when it snapped.
- **Weimar hyperinflation** — World War I prints money off the gold leash, and 1923 Germany shows the world what a dying currency looks like: wheelbarrows of cash for a loaf of bread.
- **Bretton Woods (1944)** — the most ambitious monetary deal ever struck, where 44 nations pegged their currencies to the dollar and the dollar to gold, and invented the IMF and World Bank to police it.
- **The 1971 Nixon shock** — the Sunday night a TV broadcast quietly ended gold convertibility, ending official dollar–gold convertibility, followed by the move of major currencies to floating rates in 1973.
- **Electronic money** — the eurodollar market, wire transfers, credit cards and ATMs that expanded access to money already recorded in bank ledgers.
- **Bitcoin's origins** — after the **2008 financial crisis** shattered trust in banks, the 2009 genesis block makes money-as-**code** real.
