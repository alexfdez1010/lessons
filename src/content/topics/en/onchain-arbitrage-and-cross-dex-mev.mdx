One pool offers a token more cheaply than another. Buying in the first and selling in the second changes both prices, so the visible spread is not the profit on an unlimited trade. The trade must also cover pool fees, gas and the cost of getting included.

Starting from AMM curves and transaction ordering, the course follows atomic arbitrage through sizing, settlement and competition. Atomic execution can remove exposure between successful legs; it does not remove contract, inclusion or transaction-cost risk:

- **The on-chain arb mindset** — relative value in spot AMM examples with atomic settlement, compared with inventory-based statistical arbitrage.
- **Cross-DEX price discrepancies** — how independent AMM curves drift apart, the exact constant-product math of buying cheap and selling dear, and why the optimal trade size is finite, not "as big as possible".
- **Triangular arbitrage** — closing a loop of three pools when the product of the exchange rates drifts away from 1, with the no-arbitrage condition worked out.
- **Atomic bundles & flash loans** — packaging every leg into one all-or-nothing transaction, and borrowing the entire stake inside that transaction subject to available liquidity, fees and repayment within the transaction.
- **The cost stack & the auction** — gas, priority fees, the builder's cut and slippage; backrunning vs sandwiching; and the uncomfortable result that competition bids most of the spread straight through to builders and validators.
- **Edge, capacity & competition** — the statistical edge of on-chain arb, how available liquidity, latency and the PBS supply chain limit executable opportunities.
