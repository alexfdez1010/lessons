You trade more aggressively on days when prices are already moving quickly, then observe higher execution costs. How much did your aggression cause, and how much would have happened anyway? **Causal inference** makes that counterfactual question explicit.

A signal can be useful for prediction without causing the return it predicts. Causal methods become necessary when you ask what an intervention would change. Building on the modelling and evaluation prerequisites, the course studies the assumptions needed to estimate such effects in signals and execution.

- **Prediction and causation** — why an out-of-sample association does not by itself establish an intervention’s effect, when prediction is sufficient for a decision, and how regime, liquidity, crowding and selection can confound a causal claim.
- **Potential outcomes & causal DAGs** — the Rubin potential-outcomes framework (treated vs untreated worlds, the fundamental problem of causal inference), causal directed acyclic graphs as the grammar of assumptions, and the all-important distinction between a **confounder** (a common cause you must control) and a **collider** (a common effect you must *not*).
- **Confounding & the control trap** — why "control for everything" is amateur advice: **collider bias**, **bad controls** (conditioning on a mediator or a post-treatment variable), the back-door criterion, and a clean re-reading of the factor-zoo overfitting problem as a confounding-and-multiple-comparisons problem.
- **Natural experiments & difference-in-differences** — markets rarely grant you a randomised trial, but they hand you quasi-experiments constantly: index reconstitutions, regulatory shocks, tick-size changes, index inclusions. Event studies done right, and **difference-in-differences** with its parallel-trends assumption.
- **Instrumental variables & regression discontinuity** — when you cannot block the back door, find a lever that moves the treatment but nothing else: the **instrumental-variables** logic (relevance + exclusion), two-stage estimation, and **regression discontinuity** around a sharp cutoff (index-membership rank, rating thresholds).
- **Double machine learning** — how to keep your gradient-boosted and deep models *and* estimate a causal effect under identification and nuisance-estimation assumptions: **Neyman orthogonality**, cross-fitting, partialling out high-dimensional confounders with ML nuisance models, and why naive "throw the treatment into the feature set" plug-in estimates are biased.
- **Market impact as causal TCA** — the flagship application: your trade *moves* the price, so the realised cost is a treatment effect; naive TCA is confounded because you trade more aggressively exactly when the market is already moving; the link to optimal execution and the **honest limits** — unobserved confounders, untestable assumptions, and why a true RCT is a luxury markets almost never grant.
