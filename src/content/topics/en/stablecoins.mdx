A token targets a price of one dollar, but a buyer on an exchange still needs someone willing to pay that dollar. Reserves, redemption rights and arbitrage connect the market price to the target. A disruption in any of those links can break the peg.

The course compares fiat-backed, crypto-backed and algorithmic designs, then examines depegs and regulation. The name stablecoin describes a target; the [Federal Reserve's overview of stabilization mechanisms](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-stable-in-stablecoins-20221216.html) explains why designs have different vulnerabilities:

- **What a peg is** — how redemption and arbitrage can pull the market price towards its target.
- **Fiat-backed coins** — USDT and USDC, where the whole question is *what's really in the reserve*.
- **Crypto-backed dollars** — DAI, minted as over-collateralized loans against locked crypto and policed by liquidations.
- **Algorithmic coins** — supply adjustments and the feedback loop behind Terra UST's collapse.
- **Depegs** — the USDC episode around Silicon Valley Bank's failure, and the difference between reserve, redemption and market-liquidity risk.
- **The rulebooks** — compare the scope and conditions of the **GENIUS Act** and **MiCA**.
