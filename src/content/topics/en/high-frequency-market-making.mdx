A market maker posts a bid and an ask. A trader accepts one quote, leaving the maker with inventory whose value can change before it is offset. If the trader had better information, the apparent spread can disappear into an adverse price move.

Building on microstructure and execution, this course examines quoting as a balance between spread capture, inventory and adverse selection. It then adds queue position, speed and exchange fees to the models used to choose and adjust quotes.

- **The market-making P&L** — a three-part framework (spread capture − adverse selection − inventory cost) that says whether a quoting business actually makes money.
- **The Avellaneda–Stoikov model** — the canonical optimal-quoting framework: the **reservation price**, the **optimal spread**, and how risk aversion, volatility, and time-to-close set them.
- **Inventory risk & quote skewing** — why the maker shades *both* quotes toward the side that flattens it, encouraging trades that reduce inventory without guaranteeing they will arrive.
- **Adverse selection & Glosten–Milgrom** — the model where the spread exists *purely* because some traders are informed, and quotes update with the information their orders reveal.
- **Queue position & order-book dynamics** — why *where you stand* in the queue is half the game, and how fill probability and the cancel/replace race actually work.
- **Latency & the speed arms race** — colocation, microwave towers, sniping, and the billion-dollar question of whether being one microsecond faster is worth it.
- **Maker-taker & rebate economics** — how exchange fee schedules, rebates, and tiers quietly reshape where and how makers quote.
