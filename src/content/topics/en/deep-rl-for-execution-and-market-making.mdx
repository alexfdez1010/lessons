An execution agent can reduce its reported trading cost by leaving difficult shares unfilled if its reward barely penalises unfinished orders. The network may be optimising exactly what you asked for while failing the actual trading task. **Deep reinforcement learning** therefore requires a careful state, action, reward and environment design.

This course combines the reinforcement-learning, deep-learning and market-making prerequisites. It develops value and policy methods, then compares their behaviour with execution and quoting baselines under realistic simulator limitations.

- **From tabular control to deep function approximation** — why you cannot keep a lookup table for an order book, what a neural network replaces it with, and the two canonical problems (execution, market making) with their analytic baselines (Almgren–Chriss, Avellaneda–Stoikov) that every learned agent must beat to justify itself.
- **Deep Q-networks and their instability** — the value-based family with a neural network: experience replay, target networks, and the deadly triad and the conditions that can destabilise off-policy value learning.
- **Policy gradients and actor–critic** — learning the policy directly: REINFORCE and its punishing variance, baselines and advantage, A2C and PPO, and why continuous quoting actions push trading toward policy methods.
- **Deep RL for optimal execution** — the state/action/reward design for slicing an order, how to compare a learned schedule with TWAP/VWAP and Almgren–Chriss when a signal is available, and how reward shaping silently rewrites the policy you thought you specified.
- **Deep RL for market making** — quoting as continuous control, the Avellaneda–Stoikov skeleton as the baseline, inventory-penalised spread capture, adverse selection, and the minefield of perverse incentives a market-making reward hides.
- **The sim-to-real gap and honest evaluation** — why a learned agent that prints money in your simulator loses it live: imperfect impact models, non-stationarity, overfitting the simulator, and the deflated-Sharpe / purged-cross-validation discipline that keeps the verdict honest.
