Two funds can earn the same total return while holding very different exposures. A **factor model** relates returns to shared drivers, such as the market, size or value, and estimates what remains unexplained by the chosen model. That residual is model-dependent; calling it alpha does not establish skill.

The course moves from CAPM to multi-factor construction and regression, then examines the evidence needed to distinguish a useful factor from a result of repeated testing.

- **From CAPM to factors** — which anomalies broke the one-factor world, and what a "factor" even is.
- **The Fama–French three-factor model** — adds size and value, and how SMB and HML come from a 2×3 sort of every stock on the exchange.
- **Momentum and the five-factor model** — the strongest, scariest factor (momentum, with its rare catastrophic crashes), plus profitability and investment, and the q-factor challenger.
- **Estimating factor exposures** — the econometrics: time-series regressions for an asset's loadings and alpha, plus cross-sectional and Fama–MacBeth two-pass tests of whether a factor is *priced* at all.
- **Alpha vs factor exposure** — decompose a real fund's return into factor contributions plus a residual, and examine why beating a market index alone does not establish skill.
- **Smart beta and the factor zoo** — multiple testing across published factors, implementation costs, and evidence of weakening returns after discovery or publication.
