A note offers a coupon, but payment depends on whether an index crosses a level on specified dates. To understand what you own, you need the observation schedule, redemption rules and downside payoff, not just the coupon. **Exotic options** and **structured products** make those conditions part of the contract.

Building on option pricing, Greeks and volatility, the course decomposes representative products into bond and option exposures. It keeps issuer credit risk, hedging limitations and transaction costs alongside the payoff calculations.

- **Digital (binary) options** — all-or-nothing payoffs, why a digital is the limit of a tight **call spread**, and the violent **discontinuous delta** that makes them a hedging headache near the strike.
- **Barrier options** — define knock-in and knock-out events, use in–out parity under matched cash-flow terms, and distinguish a terminal payoff discontinuity from earlier value and hedging risk.
- **Path-dependent options** — **Asian** options that settle on the *average* (cheaper, harder to manipulate) and **lookbacks** that pay off the best price you never had to time.
- **Autocallables & cliquets** — retail structures decoded observation by observation: autocall barrier, coupon barrier, **memory** coupons, and the deep **knock-in put** you're really short.
- **Structured notes, decomposed** — representative notes as a **bond + option exposures**, with the cash-flow and credit assumptions made explicit. Principal-protected notes, participation rates, and the **reverse convertible** that's just a bond minus a put.
- **Payoff engineering & hidden costs** — how desks assemble bespoke payoffs, where the **markup, bid–ask, dividend and funding assumptions** hide, the **issuer credit risk** you take on, and how to judge whether a product is worth it.

- **Monitoring and gaps** — observation calendars, equality and rebates change barrier cash flows. Separate contractual events, share-exit execution and model-conditional Brownian-bridge probabilities.
