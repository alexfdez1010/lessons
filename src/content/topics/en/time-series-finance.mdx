After several quiet trading days, a large move is followed by more large moves. A model for the average return and a model for its changing variance answer different questions. Fitting either one requires preserving time order so future information cannot leak into the past.

Building on stochastic processes and statistics, the course turns price observations into estimates, forecasts and tests on later data:

- **Stationarity & unit roots** — test whether a **price** series has a unit root, assess the behaviour of **returns**, and examine what logs and differencing change using the **augmented Dickey–Fuller test**.
- **Autocorrelation, ACF & PACF** — the fingerprint readers that say whether today's return predicts tomorrow's, judged against the **white-noise band** ($\pm 2/\sqrt{n}$) and confirmed with the **Ljung–Box test**.
- **AR, MA & ARIMA(p,d,q)** — the workhorse mean models (own past values, past shocks, and their combination), with order chosen honestly via AIC/BIC and validated on the residuals.
- **Volatility clustering & ARCH/GARCH** — modelling conditional variance: the **ARCH** insight, the three-parameter **GARCH(1,1)**, and the **persistence** $\alpha + \beta$ that sets how slowly a volatility shock fades.
- **EWMA & RiskMetrics** — the one-parameter cousin behind J.P. Morgan's Value-at-Risk numbers, and how the historical daily convention $\lambda = 0.94$ weights recent observations and compares with a fitted GARCH.
- **Backtesting traps** — the three deadliest ways to fool yourself (**look-ahead bias**, **overfitting**, **data-snooping**) and the discipline that defends against them: parsimony, out-of-sample holdouts, and **walk-forward analysis**.
