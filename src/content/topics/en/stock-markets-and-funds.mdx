You submit an order to buy a share, but the displayed price, the execution price and the cash needed at settlement are not always the same thing. A broker routes the order, a venue matches it and the trade still has to settle. A fund adds another layer by holding investments on your behalf.

After Investing Basics, this course follows that process and explains how orders, indices, funds and costs fit together:

- **The plumbing** — exchanges, brokers, market makers, and who actually gets your money
  when you buy an existing share in the secondary market.
- **Orders** — market, limit, and stop orders, the bid–ask spread, and how to avoid
  paying for your own impatience.
- **Indices** — what "the S&P 500 is up 1%" really measures, cap-weighting, and why
  the index is more top-heavy than its name suggests.
- **Funds** — ETFs vs mutual funds vs index funds, untangled for good.
- **Fees** — how an annual expense ratio reduces compounding over a specified horizon.
- **Corporate actions** — dividends, buybacks, splits, and why none of them are free money.
- **IPOs** — how companies go public, who wins the first-day pop, and who pays for it.
