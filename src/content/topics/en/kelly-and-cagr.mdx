Start with \$100, gain 50%, then lose 50%: you finish with \$75. The average of the two percentage changes is zero, but your capital has shrunk by a quarter. Position size changes this compounding path even when the underlying opportunity stays the same.

Kelly sizing maximizes expected logarithmic growth under a specified model. The model needs probabilities, payoffs and constraints; estimation errors matter as much as the formula:

- **Arithmetic vs geometric mean** — why +50% then −50% averages to 0% yet loses you 25%, and why only the geometric mean actually compounds.
- **CAGR and volatility effects** — exact factor products and log-to-simple conversion, a specified two-return family, qualified variance approximations, break-even boundaries and the difference between endpoint growth and interim drawdowns.
- **The Kelly criterion** — bet $f^* = p - q/b$ of your bankroll, the fraction that maximizes the expected *log* of your wealth (and thus its long-run growth).
- **Continuous Kelly** — derive exact excess log growth in the self-financing diffusion, distinguish absolute and cash-relative growth, and solve again when leverage caps or borrowing spreads change the objective.
- **Fractional Kelly** — the true unconstrained model identity, its limits for estimated or binary sizing, and the difference between finite positive ideal wealth, initial stopping floors and a debt-financed jump.
- **Multi-asset Kelly** — derive the covariance-aware diffusion exposure vector and cash balance, distinguish gross from net exposure, solve again under funding and position constraints, and separate relative growth, finite jumps and practical stopping needs.
