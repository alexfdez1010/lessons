A company issues a **bond**: it borrows from investors and promises payments under stated terms. Investors can often sell the bond before it matures, but the resale price may differ from what they paid. For a plain fixed-rate bond, the promised coupons stay fixed while the yield buyers require changes.

That difference explains the price–yield relationship. The course builds from the bond’s cash flows to the yield curve and the measures used to estimate interest-rate sensitivity.

- **What a bond actually is** — issuer, creditor, principal, coupon and maturity, with explicit model assumptions and examples of default recovery, sale, currency and purchasing power.
- **Bond prices and yields** — how the price of an unchanged scheduled stream responds to the required discount yield, and why promised yield differs from an actual outcome.
- **The yield curve** — define the rate and security universe, compare selected maturities, and separate expected rates, possibly negative term premia and conditional recession signals.
- **Duration** — Macaulay duration as a weighted timing measure, and modified duration as an estimate of price sensitivity to yield.
- **Convexity** — the second-order correction to duration’s straight-line price estimate.
