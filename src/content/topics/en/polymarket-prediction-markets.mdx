A YES share costs 62 cents and pays \$1 if its contract resolves YES, or zero if it resolves NO. Ignoring costs and discounting, 62% is the break-even probability at that purchase price. It is a market-implied forecast, not proof that the event has exactly that chance.

The course connects binary payouts with order books, resolution rules and position sizing. Its \$1 examples isolate the mechanism; [Polymarket's position documentation](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/positions-tokens) describes the current collateral and contract systems:

- **Price and implied probability** — how a binary payout connects price with a forecast, and how fees, spreads and risk preferences affect the interpretation.
- **Inside Polymarket** — collateral-backed outcome positions, **complete sets** (one YES + one NO), and the split, merge and redemption lifecycle.
- **The CLOB** — the central limit order book where YES shares trade, with its spread, depth, and slippage.
- **Resolution** — contract rules, UMA proposal and dispute procedures for prediction markets, and separate mechanisms such as Chainlink TWAP up/down markets. A correct view about the world still needs to match the contract payout.
- **Calibration & the favorite–longshot bias** — score the market like any forecaster: do events priced at 30¢ happen 30% of the time? Examine when longshot and favourite prices show systematic bias.
- **Binary arbitrage** — compare a complete set with its collateral value, including executable prices, fees and settlement constraints.
- **Beliefs, costs and Kelly sizing** — distinguish net shares, funded capital and redemption notional; model uncertain payout beliefs and use executable all-in costs.
- **Joint and practical risks** — explicit resolution states, correlated exposures, liquidity, settlement time, collateral and current access rules, without assuming every risk is absent from price.
