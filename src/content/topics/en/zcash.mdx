A public payment ledger reveals addresses and amounts, even when those addresses do not show a person's name. Zcash adds shielded transfers: zero-knowledge proofs let the network verify a payment without publishing its protected sender, receiver and amount.

Shielding is a transaction property, not a guarantee that every use of Zcash is anonymous. Following the distinction between [shielded and transparent activity](https://z.cash/learn/is-zcash-traceable/), the course examines what stays visible and how information outside a payment can reveal a link:

- **Why Bitcoin leaks** — how public addresses and amounts can be linked to identities through additional information.
- **Private money, defined** — what "privacy" actually means for payments, and what it doesn't.
- **Zero-knowledge proofs** — the cryptography that proves a payment is valid while hiding its details.
- **Inside the Zcash machine** — how the protocol bolts privacy onto a verifiable public ledger.
- **A shielded transaction** — walking through a shielded payment end to end.
- **De-anonymizing mistakes** — the slip-ups that quietly unmask people who thought they were safe.
