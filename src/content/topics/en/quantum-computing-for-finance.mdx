A claimed speedup counts calls to a quantum operator, while a business comparison needs elapsed time and total resources. Preparing the input state, running a circuit and reading the result all belong in that comparison. Leaving them out can make a theoretical advantage look like a practical one.

Building on Monte Carlo and portfolio optimization, this course examines amplitude estimation, discrete optimization and quantum-inspired methods. It separates algorithmic results under stated assumptions from evidence that a complete financial workflow beats a classical baseline:

- **Quantum foundations for finance** — qubits, superposition, entanglement and gates explained for the quant, not the physicist; and the two hardware paradigms you'll keep meeting: the **gate model** (universal, runs algorithms like QAE/QAOA) versus **quantum annealing** (special-purpose, solves one kind of optimization problem).
- **Amplitude estimation and Monte Carlo** — a conditional query-complexity result for bounded expectations, with explicit payoff scaling, probability/error units, confidence, state preparation and resource budgets; tail quantiles and ES need their own conventions.
- **QUBO & portfolio optimization** — recasting asset selection as **Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization**, solved by **QAOA** on the gate model or **quantum annealing** on D-Wave. The focus is **discrete, cardinality-constrained** selection (pick exactly $k$ names, lot-size constraints), compared with suitable classical discrete solvers.
- **Quantum machine learning for signals** — quantum kernels and variational classifiers for alpha, taught with the heaviest skepticism in the course: the theory is elegant, the finance evidence is thin, and the data-loading wall is brutal.
- **The reality check: NISQ & data loading** — the walls. Today's **noisy intermediate-scale quantum** machines decohere before deep circuits finish; **error correction** requires substantial, architecture-dependent physical-qubit overhead; and above all the **data-loading / state-preparation** problem can quietly erase your quadratic speedup before the algorithm even starts.
- **Quantum-inspired classical methods** — the punchline that pays rent *today*: **tensor networks** and other quantum-inspired algorithms that run on the classical hardware in your data center right now, and the discipline of judging any quantum claim **end-to-end** — loading and readout included — against the best classical baseline.
