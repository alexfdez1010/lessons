A share you bought for \$100 now trades at \$70. You refuse to sell until it returns to your purchase price, even though you would not buy it today. The \$100 is influencing a decision that should depend on what the investment offers from here.

Behavioural finance studies patterns like this. Using the work of Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky and other researchers, the course connects each bias to a decision, the evidence behind it and the limits of that evidence:

- **Your two minds** — the fast, automatic gut and the slow, effortful checker, and why markets
  are a hostile place for intuition.
- **Prospect theory** — how you really feel gains and losses: reference points, loss aversion,
  diminishing sensitivity, and the strange way we weight probabilities.
- **Sunk costs and the disposition effect** — why we sell winners, marry losers, and throw good
  money after bad.
- **Overconfidence and overtrading** — the calibration gap, the illusion of control, and the
  measurable bill that hyperactive trading runs up.
- **Anchoring, framing and mental accounting** — how an irrelevant number, a re-worded fact, or
  a mental "bucket" hijacks a decision.
- **Availability, representativeness and base rates** — judging by what springs to mind or what
  resembles a stereotype, and the gambler's-fallacy trap.
- **Confirmation bias and narrative** — the stories we tell to defend what we already believe,
  and how to write a thesis that can actually be proven wrong.
- **Herding, FOMO and bubbles** — how crowds, cascades and fear of missing out inflate manias
  (and why going contrarian is no free win).
- **Survivorship and selection bias** — why the funds, backtests and gurus you see can be a selected
  sample, and how to vet a track record.
- **A debiasing toolkit** — checklists, premortems, the outside view, a decision journal, sell
  rules and automation, and the discipline of judging process over outcome.
