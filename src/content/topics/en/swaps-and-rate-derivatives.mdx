A company pays floating interest on a loan and wants more predictable payments. In a plain-vanilla same-currency swap, it can pay a fixed rate and receive a floating rate on an agreed notional amount. The notional sets the scale of the interest calculation; it is not normally exchanged in this type of swap.

Using bond valuation, curves and DV01, the course builds the cash flows, values the contract and examines what a rate hedge can leave uncovered:

- **What a swap is** — the fixed leg, the floating leg, when matching payment dates allow net settlement, and the two classic motives: transforming a liability and exploiting comparative advantage.
- **FRAs & forward rates** — the forward rate agreement, the single-period atom of a swap, and the insight that a swap is just a **strip of FRAs** bolted together.
- **Pricing swaps as bond differences** — value a swap as a fixed-rate bond minus a floating-rate bond, identify when the textbook floating-rate bond is worth par at reset, and derive the **par swap rate** that makes a new swap worth zero.
- **The swap curve & OIS discounting** — build the term structure of par swap rates, read the implied forwards inside it, and discount cash flows on a curve consistent with the collateral agreement, including **OIS** discounting.
- **SOFR & the death of LIBOR** — why a benchmark that underpinned hundreds of trillions was scrapped, and how a **secured, overnight, compounded-in-arrears** rate replaced it.
- **Swap spreads & asset swaps** — the gap between swap rates and government yields, why the long end can go **negative**, and how an asset swap strips a bond down to a clean spread over the floating index.
- **Caps, floors & swaptions** — the optional cousins: a cap that bounds your floating cost, a floor that protects a lender, and a swaption — the option to enter a swap.
- **Hedging a rate book with swaps** — the payoff of the whole course: neutralise the DV01 of an entire portfolio with a swap overlay and examine the remaining curve, basis, convexity and counterparty risks.
