Suppose an account needs at least \$500 to continue trading. Starting at \$1,000, it can cross that limit during a losing run even if the strategy has positive expected profit. A favourable average does not tell you whether the account survives the path.

This course makes the failure threshold explicit, then connects trade size, payoff distributions, withdrawals and drawdowns to the probability of crossing it:

- **What ruin actually is** — the absorbing barrier (an account too small to keep trading), the classic *gambler's ruin* result that even a fair game bankrupts a finite purse, and the paradox that a winning system can still ruin you.
- **Expectancy and the edge** — the per-trade engine, expectancy = (average win × win rate) − (average loss × loss rate), and how a 35% win rate can still have positive expectancy when wins are sufficiently large relative to losses.
- **R-multiples** — the elegant bookkeeping that normalizes every trade to a single unit of risk.
- **The classic risk-of-ruin formula** — how edge, odds, and the number of *units of risk* in your bankroll combine into a ruin probability that falls *exponentially* with capital in the model, with fully worked examples.
- **Drawdown distributions** — depth, duration, and the dreaded *time under water* treated not as single numbers but as random variables; the same system run twice gives two very different worst-cases.
- **Sequencing risk** — without cashflows the *order* of returns is irrelevant, but add withdrawals and order becomes destiny: early losses can force withdrawals from a depleted balance and change the survival outcome.
- **Monte Carlo ruin curves and stops** — estimating ruin by brute force, plotting ruin against risk-per-trade, and the stop-loss arithmetic that turns a risk budget into a planned position size whose actual loss also depends on gaps and execution.
