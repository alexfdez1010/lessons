You have \$1,000 and two different needs: a bill due next month and a goal ten years away. Keeping money accessible helps with the bill; investing may help with the distant goal, but the value can fall when you need it. Choosing starts with the job you need the money to do.

No knowledge of financial products is assumed. The lessons explain what you own, how it might pay you and what can go wrong:

- **What an asset is** — the thing you're buying when you invest, and the two ways it can pay you.
- **Saving vs investing** — why they're not the same job, and which one to do first.
- **Risk and return** — the trade-off behind *every* investment decision, and why there's no free lunch.
- **Stocks, bonds, and funds** — a plain-English tour of the three things beginners actually buy.
- **What "a return" really means** — so a percentage on a screen stops being mysterious.
- **Custody, ownership and asset segregation** — distinguish issuer, investor interest, custodian and records; separate market losses from missing units, with explicitly scoped Spanish protection examples.
