If \$100 earns 5% over one year, it becomes \$105. Under the same rate and assumptions, receiving \$105 in a year is equivalent to receiving \$100 today. Changing the rate changes that comparison; receiving a future payment also depends on whether it is actually paid.

The course uses this forward-and-back calculation to introduce the time value of money before applying it to later topics:

- **Compounding** — how today's money snowballs forward over time.
- **Future value** — what a sum you hold now will be worth later.
- **Discounting** — how tomorrow's money shrinks back to today.
- **Present value** — what a future payment is really worth right now.
- **The discount rate** — the dial that controls how fast that shrinking happens.

The cash-flow timeline lesson then combines receipts and payments at one valuation date. A final exam checks both single-payment conversions and dated schedules.

The equal-payment lesson derives finite ordinary and due annuities from the dated sum, including future values at a common date and the zero-rate case.
