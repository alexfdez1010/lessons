If \$100 earns 5% over one year, it becomes \$105. Under the same rate and assumptions, receiving \$105 in a year is equivalent to receiving \$100 today. Changing the rate changes that comparison; receiving a future payment also depends on whether it is actually paid.

The course builds from one payment to repeated payment schedules:

- **Present and future value** move a single amount between dates using compounding or discounting.
- **Cash-flow timelines** put signed receipts and payments on one valuation date before adding them.
- **Finite annuities** derive ordinary and due payment formulas, match their dates and handle a zero rate by direct addition.
- **Perpetuities and growing payments** extend partial sums indefinitely, locate the first payment and test whether the positive stream has a finite limit.

- **Net present value** compares signed value added with an explicit opportunity-cost rate, budget and cash-flow assumptions.

- **Variable rates and linked discount factors** give each interval its own growth factor, convert signed payments between dates, and separate an equivalent endpoint rate from external-flow timing.

The final exam checks all six strands, including the assumptions that make each comparison valid.
