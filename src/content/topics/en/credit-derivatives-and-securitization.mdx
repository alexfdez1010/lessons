A lender can keep a loan, buy protection against specified credit events, or pool loans and issue securities against their cash flows. Each choice distributes losses differently. A **credit default swap (CDS)** transfers defined credit risk; **securitization** allocates a pool’s payments and losses across securities with different priorities.

This course builds on bonds and swaps to price that protection and trace the loss allocation. It examines the role of default correlation, the failures exposed in 2008 and the coverage tests used in collateralized loan obligations (CLOs).

- **What a CDS is** — the premium leg, the protection leg, the specified ISDA credit events that can trigger settlement, and how the "Big Bang" standardized the whole market into fixed coupons and auctions.
- **Spreads & hazard rates** — turn a CDS spread into a *probability of default* with the **credit triangle** ($\text{spread} \approx \lambda(1-R)$), build a survival curve, and mark a CDS to market with its risky annuity.
- **CDS indices** — how **CDX** and **iTraxx** bundle reference entities into one tradable spread, the six-month **roll**, and the **basis** between the index and the names inside it.
- **Securitization** — pool loans into a bankruptcy-remote **SPV**, issue bonds against them, and meet the **MBS** and **ABS** that result, prepayment risk and all.
- **Tranching & waterfalls** — slice the pool by **attachment/detachment points**, pay the cash out through a **waterfall**, and see how **subordination** and other credit enhancement turn mediocre collateral into a fat AAA tranche.
- **Correlation & 2008** — how joint defaults affect tranche losses, the **Gaussian copula** that modelled it, and the precise way mis-estimating it vaporized "safe" senior tranches, AIG, and the monolines.
- **CLOs today** — the modern, actively managed cousin built from leveraged loans, the **OC/IC coverage tests** that force-deleverage it in a downturn, and the remaining credit, model and liquidity risks despite senior protection.
