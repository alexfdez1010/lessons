Put \$1,000 in an account paying 5% for a year and you have \$1,050 before fees and tax. If prices rise 6%, that larger balance buys less than your original \$1,000. The rate tells only part of the story: you also need its period, how interest is added and what happens to prices.

Starting from the time value of money, this course separates those questions:

- **Simple vs compound interest** — earning on your principal versus earning on your interest too, and why *how often* it compounds changes the answer.
- **Compounding frequency** — why the same rate pays out differently depending on the schedule.
- **Nominal and effective annual rates** — separate compounding from fees, then distinguish these calculations from regulatory APR and APY disclosures.
- **Rate conventions** — what a quoted rate includes, which costs remain separate and why the jurisdiction matters.
- **Nominal vs real** — stripping out inflation to see what your money can actually *buy*.

- **Day counts and interest accrual** — count actual dates, apply a specified year-fraction convention and keep simple accrual separate from capitalization and payment timing.
