You hedge an option’s delta with the underlying. The price then moves, changing the option’s delta through gamma, while time and implied volatility also change its value. Keeping a hedge useful requires tracking those exposures and paying attention to the cost of rebalancing.

Building on Options Pricing, the course applies the Greeks to portfolio management, volatility surfaces and gamma scalping. Profit-and-loss attribution is treated as an approximation whose residual can reveal higher-order moves, model differences and costs.

- **Delta & gamma** — measure current stock exposure, combine position signs and understand why price, time and volatility change the share hedge.
- **Theta & rho** — the clock Greeks: the rent you pay for holding optionality, and the quiet pull of interest rates.
- **The gamma–theta tradeoff** — interpret curvature and time change together, with financing and dividend assumptions stated.
- **Vega & the volatility surface** — implied vol is a landscape, bending across strike (**skew** and **smile**) and maturity (**term structure**); your book is long or short vega at every point.
- **Delta-neutral hedging** — maintain signed share and cash accounts, distinguish full repricing from Taylor and choose a policy with an explicit risk objective and costs.
- **Gamma scalping** — track the rebalancing of long-gamma portfolios and assess weighted realised moves, option marks, financing and execution costs.
- **P&L attribution** — split a day's change into delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho pieces and interpret the approximation’s residual and trading costs.
